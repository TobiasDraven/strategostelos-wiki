# Platoon Commander's Landward Operations — Manoeuvre and Combined Arms at the Tactical Level

> *"The platoon leader must have a relatively complete knowledge of the terrain, and he must know as much as possible about the enemy."*
> — FM 3-21.8, *Infantry Platoon and Squad*

The platoon commander operates at the tactical level where intent is translated into action. The platoon commander receives the company commander's intent and must achieve it through the coordinated action of multiple sections. This requires mastery of manoeuvre and combined arms at a higher level than the section commander.

This page discusses the landward domain at the platoon and company level, relevant to lieutenants and captains.

---

## The Manoeuvrist Approach

The manoeuvrist approach is an indirect approach that applies strength against enemy vulnerabilities. It blends lethal and non-lethal actions to shape the enemy's understanding, undermine their will, and break their cohesion.

The manoeuvrist approach is suited to a small, professional force. It avoids attritional warfare — the grinding destruction of enemy forces through superior firepower. Instead, it seeks to dislocate and disintegrate the enemy through speed, surprise, and the concentration of force at the decisive point.

### Shaping the Enemy's Understanding

The manoeuvrist approach seeks to shape what the enemy perceives. Deception, feints, and indirect approaches confuse the enemy about the location and timing of the main effort. The enemy is induced to make mistakes — to commit reserves to the wrong location, to expose flanks, or to hesitate at the critical moment.

### Breaking Cohesion

The manoeuvrist approach seeks to break the enemy's cohesion — the ability of the enemy force to act as a unified whole. Cohesion is broken by creating multiple dilemmas simultaneously. The enemy is forced to choose between responding to one threat or another. Whichever choice the enemy makes, they become vulnerable to the other threat.

## Combined Arms at the Platoon Level

Combined arms is the synchronised and simultaneous application of tactical forces and capabilities to create an effect greater than if each element was used separately. At the platoon level, combined arms means the integration of infantry, armour, artillery, engineers, and aviation.

### Infantry

Infantry is the primary arm of land warfare. Infantry can operate in any terrain. Infantry can close with the enemy and destroy or capture them at close range. Infantry can hold ground against counterattack.

### Armour

Armour provides mobile protected firepower. Tanks and infantry fighting vehicles can destroy enemy armour, suppress enemy positions, and break through enemy defences. Armour is most effective in open terrain where its mobility and firepower can be fully exploited.

### Artillery

Artillery provides indirect fire support. Mortars and field guns can suppress the enemy, destroy enemy positions, and provide smoke and illumination. Artillery is the primary means of shaping the battlefield before the assault.

### Engineers

Engineers provide mobility and counter-mobility. Engineers breach obstacles, clear minefields, and construct defences. Engineers enable the force to move through denied terrain and prevent the enemy from moving through key terrain.

### Aviation

Aviation provides reconnaissance, fire support, and mobility. Attack helicopters can destroy enemy armour and suppress enemy positions. Transport helicopters can insert troops into denied terrain. Unmanned aerial systems provide real-time intelligence.

## The Platoon Attack

The platoon attack is a combined arms operation. The platoon commander coordinates the actions of sections, supporting weapons, and adjacent units to achieve the objective.

### The Plan

The platoon commander develops a plan that includes:

- **Mission analysis:** What is the mission? What are the key factors? What are the enemy's likely courses of action?

- **Scheme of manoeuvre:** How will the platoon achieve the objective? What is the main effort? What is the supporting effort?

- **Fire support plan:** How will supporting weapons support the attack? What are the targets? When will fires be lifted?

- **Coordinating instructions:** What are the control measures? What are the rules of engagement? What is the timeline?

### The Preparation

The platoon commander prepares the platoon for the attack. Preparation includes:

- **Reconnaissance:** The platoon commander conducts a reconnaissance of the objective and the routes to the objective.

- **Briefing:** The platoon commander briefs section commanders on the plan. Every section commander must understand the mission, the scheme of manoeuvre, and the fire support plan.

- **Rehearsal:** The platoon rehearses the attack. Rehearsals identify problems and build confidence.

### The Execution

The platoon commander controls the attack through fire and movement. The platoon commander designates the main effort and coordinates supporting fires. The platoon commander adjusts the plan as the situation changes.

### Consolidation

The platoon consolidates on the objective. Consolidation includes reorganising the platoon, redistributing ammunition, and preparing for counterattack. Consolidation is often neglected but is critical to holding ground.

## The Platoon Defence

The platoon defence is a combined arms operation. The platoon commander positions sections to maximise firepower while minimising exposure. The platoon commander coordinates supporting weapons to destroy the enemy before they reach the platoon's positions.

### The Plan

The platoon commander develops a defensive plan that includes:

- **Security:** Early warning of the enemy's approach. Observation posts and patrols.

- **Main battle area:** The positions where the platoon will fight. Primary, alternate, and supplementary positions.

- **Fire support plan:** The integration of direct and indirect fires. Target reference points. Final protective fires.

- **Reserve:** A section held back to counterattack or reinforce.

### The Preparation

The platoon commander prepares the defence. Preparation includes:

- **Position construction:** Fighting positions, trenches, and obstacles.

- **Fire planning:** Clearing fields of fire. Assigning sectors of fire.

- **Rehearsal:** Rehearsal of the defence, including the counterattack.

### The Execution

The platoon commander controls the defence. The platoon commander calls for indirect fire. The platoon commander commits the reserve at the decisive moment.

---

## The Platoon Commander's Landward Problem

The platoon commander faces a complex landward problem. The platoon commander must simultaneously manoeuvre sections, coordinate supporting weapons, and maintain situational awareness. The platoon commander must be able to shift from offence to defence quickly and to adapt to the changing situation.

The platoon commander who masters combined arms and the manoeuvrist approach is the platoon commander who wins.
