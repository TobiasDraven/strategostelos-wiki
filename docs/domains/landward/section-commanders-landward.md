# Section Commander's Landward Operations — Reading Ground, Fighting, and Holding

> *"The section is the smallest military unit capable of independent tactical action."*
> — ATP 3-21.8, *Infantry Platoon and Squad*

The section commander operates at the lowest level of military command. Decisions are immediate. The consequences are direct. The section commander must read the ground, fight the battle, and hold the ground — all simultaneously.

This page discusses the landward domain at the tactical level most relevant to junior leaders: the lance corporal and corporal commanding a fire team or section.

---

## Reading the Ground

Terrain is the physical framework within which every engagement occurs. The section commander must read terrain quickly and accurately.

### Key Terrain Features

Terrain is analysed using the military mnemonic **OCOKA**:

- **Observation:** Where can you see the enemy? Where can the enemy see you? Good observation enables effective fire and early warning. Poor observation creates surprise.

- **Cover and concealment:** Cover protects from fire. Concealment hides from observation. The section commander must identify both and use them to protect the section.

- **Obstacles:** Natural or artificial features that impede movement. Rivers, walls, minefields, wire. Obstacles can protect flanks or block routes of advance.

- **Key terrain:** Any feature whose seizure, retention, or control provides a marked advantage to either side. A hilltop. A bridge. A road junction. Key terrain is the spatial focus of the battle.

- **Avenues of attack:** Routes along which a force can move to reach its objective. The section commander must identify covered and concealed avenues that allow the section to close with the enemy.

### Ground and Movement

The section commander must calculate not merely the distance to an objective, but the time required to traverse that distance in the specific terrain. A kilometre of open ground takes minutes. A kilometre of dense vegetation takes longer. A kilometre under fire takes longest of all.

Ground also determines the formation the section adopts. Open terrain may allow a wide skirmish line. Jungle requires a single file. Urban terrain requires bounding overwatch between buildings.

---

## Fighting the Battle

The section commander fights the battle through fire and movement. Fire suppresses the enemy. Movement closes the distance. The combination of fire and movement is the fundamental tactic of land warfare.

### Fire and Movement

Fire and movement requires one element to suppress the enemy while another element moves. The section commander designates which fire team suppresses and which moves. The timing is critical. If the suppressing fire lifts too early, the moving team is exposed. If it lifts too late, the moving team cannot advance.

### Fire Control

The section commander controls fire through fire orders. Fire orders include the target indication, the rate of fire, and the duration of fire. The section commander must ensure that all members of the section understand the fire orders and can deliver accurate fire on the target.

### Close Combat

The section commander must be prepared to close with the enemy. Close combat is the defining characteristic of land warfare. The section that closes with the enemy and destroys or captures them achieves the objective.

Close combat requires aggression, training, and unit cohesion. The section commander must inspire the section to fight at close quarters and overcome the natural resistance to killing.

---

## Holding the Ground

Seizing ground is only half the battle. The section must hold the ground against counterattack. Holding requires preparation, organisation, and the will to defend.

### Hasty Defence

When the section seizes an objective, it immediately organises a hasty defence. The section commander designates primary and alternate positions. Fields of fire are cleared. Ammunition is redistributed. The section prepares for the inevitable counterattack.

### Positions

The section commander positions the section to maximise firepower while minimising exposure. Positions must provide cover from fire and observation. They must also provide fields of fire that cover the likely avenues of enemy approach.

### Sectors of Fire

The section commander assigns sectors of fire to each fire team. Sectors of fire ensure that all approaches are covered. They also prevent fratricide by ensuring that each fire team knows where friendly forces are located.

---

## The Section Commander's Landward Problem

The section commander faces a unique problem in the landward domain. The section commander must simultaneously read the ground, fight the battle, and hold the ground. These tasks are interdependent. Reading the ground enables effective fighting. Fighting enables holding the ground. Holding the ground provides the platform for the next advance.

The section commander who masters this cycle is the section commander who wins.
