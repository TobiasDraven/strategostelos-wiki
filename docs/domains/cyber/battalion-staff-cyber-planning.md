# Battalion Staff Cyber Planning — Integrating CEMA into Command

> *"CEMA synchronizes capabilities across domains and warfighting functions and maximizes complementary effects in and through cyberspace and the EMS."*
> — FM 3-12, *Cyberspace and Electronic Warfare Operations*

---

## Cyber at the Battalion Headquarters

A battalion staff plans and integrates cyberspace and electronic warfare operations through the CEMA cell. The S2 (intelligence) and S3 (operations) lead this effort, with support from the signal officer (S6) and the electronic warfare officer (EWO). At this level, cyber is not a separate function. It is integrated into every phase of the operations process.

This page covers how a battalion staff plans, integrates, and assesses CEMA operations. It focuses on the staff processes that synchronize cyber and EW with maneuver, fires, and intelligence.

---

## The CEMA Cell

### Structure

The CEMA cell is not a separate section. It is a coordinating element within the battalion staff. It includes:
- **S2.** Leads intelligence preparation of the battlefield (IPB). Identifies enemy cyber and EW capabilities.
- **S3.** Integrates CEMA effects into the scheme of maneuver. Synchronizes fires and EW.
- **S6.** Manages the battalion's network. Coordinates spectrum management.
- **EWO (if assigned).** Provides expertise on electronic attack and electronic warfare support.
- **Cyber planner (if augmented).** Embedded from the brigade or expeditionary cyber team.

### Functions

The CEMA cell performs four critical functions:
1. **Plan.** Develops the CEMA annex to the operations order.
2. **Integrate.** Synchronizes cyber and EW with the scheme of maneuver.
3. **Assess.** Monitors the electromagnetic environment and evaluates effects.
4. **Coordinate.** Liaises with higher headquarters and adjacent units.

---

## Intelligence Preparation of the Battlefield — Cyber and EW

### IPB with CEMA

The S2 conducts IPB with a focus on enemy cyber and EW capabilities. This includes:
- **Enemy cyber capabilities.** Can the enemy conduct offensive cyber operations? Can they jam our communications? Can they geolocate our emitters?
- **Enemy EW capabilities.** What electronic attack systems do they have? What direction-finding capabilities do they possess?
- **Enemy network dependencies.** What systems depend on the EMS? What are the critical nodes?
- **Cyber key terrain.** What network infrastructure provides tactical advantage?

### Situation Templates

The S2 develops situation templates that show how the enemy will employ cyber and EW capabilities in each course of action. These templates integrate with the overall enemy SITEMP and help the commander anticipate enemy actions.

## Planning

### The MDMP with CEMA

During the military decision-making process (MDMP), the CEMA cell contributes at each step:

**Mission Analysis:**
- Identify cyber and EW constraints and restraints.
- Identify cyber key terrain.
- Develop information requirements about the enemy's cyber and EW capabilities.

**Course of Action Development:**
- Include CEMA effects in each friendly course of action.
- Identify how cyber and EW support the main effort.
- Coordinate with the fire support team for lethal and non-lethal effects.

**Course of Action Analysis (Wargaming):**
- The S2 plays the enemy's cyber and EW capabilities during wargame.
- Identify decision points where CEMA effects are critical.
- Assess the risk of enemy cyber and EW attacks on friendly operations.

**Orders Production:**
- Develop the CEMA annex to the operations order.
- Include spectrum management guidance.
- Include emissions control procedures.

### METT-TC2

Use METT-TC2 to evaluate the operational environment. The second "C" (cyber) adds three layers of analysis:
- **Physical network layer.** Where are the network nodes? What is the infrastructure?
- **Logical network layer.** What are the data flows? What are the vulnerabilities?
- **Cyber-persona layer.** Who are the users? What are their access permissions?

## Synchronisation

### The CEMA Synchronisation Matrix

The S3 develops the CEMA synchronisation matrix. It shows:
- What effects are required.
- When they are required.
- Who is responsible.
- How they support the maneuver plan.

### Coordination with Fires

Cyber and EW effects are coordinated through the fires process. Electronic attack is treated like a fire support asset. It is targeted, deconflicted, and assessed like any other fire.

The fire support team coordinates:
- **Electronic attack** against enemy command and control nodes.
- **Electronic warfare support** for targeting.
- **Cyber effects** against enemy networks.

---

## Assessment

### Measuring Effects

The CEMA cell assesses the effectiveness of cyber and EW operations:
- **Were enemy networks disrupted?**
- **Were enemy communications degraded?**
- **Did friendly forces maintain freedom of maneuver in cyberspace and the EMS?**
- **What was the impact on the enemy's decision cycle?**

### Continuous Monitoring

The S2 and S6 continuously monitor the electromagnetic environment. They track changes in enemy emissions. They assess the health of friendly networks. They provide updates to the commander through running estimates.

---

## Command Post Operations

### The CEMA Cell in the TOC

The CEMA cell operates within the battalion tactical operations center (TOC). It maintains situational awareness of the electromagnetic environment. It communicates with the brigade CEMA cell. It coordinates with the scout platoon for electronic warfare support data.

### Displacement

When the TOC displaces, the CEMA cell must maintain continuity of operations. The S6 manages the network transition. The S2 maintains intelligence coverage. The S3 synchronizes the displacement of CEMA assets.
