# Cyber Resilience in ASEAN — The Singapore Model

> *"Cybersecurity is a team sport, and everyone has a part to play."*
> — Singapore Cybersecurity Strategy 2021

## The Singapore Approach

Singapore's cybersecurity model is built on three strategic pillars and two foundational enablers, as outlined in the Singapore Cybersecurity Strategy 2021.[^1] The strategy takes a proactive stance against emerging threats like quantum computing, simplifies cybersecurity for end-users, and promotes international cyber norms.

**Three Strategic Pillars:**
1. **Build Resilient Infrastructure** — Strengthening Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) across 11 sectors including government, telecommunications, energy, aviation, maritime, land transport, healthcare, banking and finance, water, security and emergency, and media.[^2]
2. **Enable a Safer Cyberspace** — Promoting involvement from government, industry, and the public through the National Cybercrime Action Plan.
3. **Enhance International Cyber Cooperation** — Championing initiatives for cyber capacity building and facilitating global exchanges on cyber norms.

**Two Foundational Enablers:**
- Develop a vibrant cybersecurity ecosystem
- Grow a robust talent pipeline

## The Cyber TIG Plan

In September 2023, the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) launched the Cybersecurity Talent, Innovation & Growth (Cyber TIG) Plan, backed by S$50 million over three years.[^3] The plan focuses on three pillars:

- **Talent**: Mid-career conversion programmes to train experienced STEM professionals for cyber roles, aiming to add 100 professionals annually.
- **Innovation**: The CyberBoost accelerator helps cybersecurity companies develop, validate, and scale solutions from Singapore.
- **Growth**: The CyberGrowth export programme assists local companies in identifying overseas market opportunities.

The CyberSG TIG Collaboration Centre, established in partnership with the National University of Singapore (NUS), serves as a national hub integrating industry, academia, and government stakeholders.[^4]

## Regional Capacity Building

Singapore has positioned itself as ASEAN's cybersecurity hub through sustained investment in regional capacity building:

- **ASEAN Cyber Capacity Programme (ACCP)**: Launched in 2016 with S$10 million over 10 years to build cyber capacity across ASEAN member states.[^2]
- **ASEAN-Singapore Cybersecurity Centre of Excellence (ASCCE)**: Established in 2019 with S$30 million in funding, delivering over 50 programmes for more than 1,500 senior officials from ASEAN and beyond.[^5]
- **Singapore International Cyber Week (SICW)**: The region's premier annual cybersecurity event, convening global thought leaders, industry experts, and policymakers.
- **UN-Singapore Cyber Fellowship**: Launched in 2021 to equip senior officials with interdisciplinary expertise in cyber and digital security policymaking.

## Legal Framework

The Cybersecurity Act 2018 provides the legal framework for implementing Singapore's cybersecurity strategy. Its four key objectives are:[^2]

1. Strengthen protection of Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) against cyber-attacks
2. Authorise CSA to prevent and respond to cybersecurity threats and incidents
3. Establish a licensing framework for cybersecurity service providers
4. Provide a framework for sharing cybersecurity information

## Lessons for ASEAN and the Developing World

Singapore's model offers several transferable lessons:

1. **Whole-of-nation approach**: Cybersecurity is not solely a government responsibility — industry and the public must participate.
2. **Proactive legal frameworks**: The Cybersecurity Act 2018 was updated to address emerging threats, including expanding CII to cover key digital infrastructure and services.
3. **Investment in talent**: The Cyber TIG Plan's focus on mid-career conversion addresses the critical cybersecurity workforce gap.
4. **Regional leadership**: Singapore's investment in ASEAN-wide capacity building demonstrates how a small state can exercise outsized influence in cybersecurity governance.
5. **International norms**: Singapore has played a leading role in both the UNGGE and OEWG on cybersecurity, advocating for a rules-based multilateral order in cyberspace.
