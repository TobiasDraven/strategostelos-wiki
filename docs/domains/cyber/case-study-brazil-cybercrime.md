# Cybercrime in Latin America: Brazil

> *"As many countries, Brazil has been dealing with cybercrime, a phenomenon which is increasing in number and sophistication. The migration of various criminal offenses to digital platforms demands decisive efforts towards updating a proper normative and law-enforcement response to the threats, including internationally."*
> — Permanent Mission of Brazil to the UN, *Brazilian Position on Cybercrime Convention*

## The Scale of the Problem

Brazil is Latin America's largest internet market, with more than 160 million users. This scale creates both opportunity and vulnerability. Cybercrime in Brazil is not a niche criminal activity; it is a major economic and social phenomenon that exploits digital infrastructure, financial systems, and human rights protections.

SaferNet Brasil's National Human Rights Cyber Hotline processed 101,313 unique reports of online rights violations in 2023. This figure represents a historic peak and includes child sexual abuse material (CSAM), hate speech, racism, religious intolerance, and homophobia. The volume reflects both the scale of harm and the growing capacity of Brazilian civil society to detect and report it.

## Institutional Architecture

Brazil has developed one of Latin America's most sophisticated multi-stakeholder cybercrime governance architectures. Three pillars structure the response:

### 1. NIC.br and CGI.br

The Núcleo de Informação e Coordenação do Ponto BR (NIC.br) operates under the Comitê Gestor da Internet no Brasil (CGI.br). CGI.br is the Brazilian Internet Steering Committee—a multi-stakeholder body that coordinates internet policy, research, and incident response. NIC.br provides technical coordination, data collection, and research on internet development and security.

NIC.br's partnership with SaferNet Brasil, formalised in 2008, created a technical backbone for the National Human Rights Cyber Hotline. NIC.br supplies connectivity, data management, and technical infrastructure; SaferNet operates the hotline and helpline; the Public Prosecutor's Office provides legal oversight.

This arrangement is unusual in Latin America. It combines technical expertise, civil society operational capacity, and state prosecutorial power in a single coordinated mechanism.

### 2. SaferNet Brasil

SaferNet is a Brazilian non-governmental organisation that operates three interlocking services:
- **National Human Rights Cyber Hotline.** Receives anonymous reports of cybercrimes targeting human rights. Works in partnership with Public Prosecutors to strengthen actions against online violations.
- **National Web-based Helpline.** Provides one-to-one online counselling for children, young people, and families about online safety.
- **Brazilian Awareness Node.** Delivers capacity-building programmes for educators, young people, legislators, policymakers, and social workers.

The hotline's 2023 statistics illustrate the demand: 71,867 new reports of CSAM alone—the highest in the hotline's 18-year history. The total of 101,313 unique reports across all violation categories represented a historic peak.

### 3. UNODC Brazil

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) operates a Brazil country office that works on cybercrime as part of its global programme. UNODC's engagement includes:
- Technical assistance for legislative reform.
- Capacity-building for law enforcement and prosecutors.
- Support for international cooperation frameworks.

In 2021, UNODC Brazil organised a workshop on "Cybersecurity, Human Rights, and Digital Citizenship" under its Education for Justice (E4J) programme and Global Cybercrime Programme. The workshop trained Brazilian youth leaders on cybercrime typologies, legal frameworks, and digital rights.

## Crime Typologies

Brazilian cybercrime exhibits several distinct patterns:

### Financial Cybercrime

Brazil has one of the world's highest rates of financial fraud. Banking trojans, phishing, and social engineering against mobile banking users are widespread. The country's advanced mobile financial ecosystem—including instant payment systems—provides both convenience and attack surface.

### Child Sexual Abuse Material

CSAM represents the largest single category of hotline reports. Brazil's position as a major social media and content market creates both production and distribution risks. SaferNet's automated detection systems, combined with human moderation, process thousands of reports daily.

### Hate Speech and Intolerance

The 2023 hotline data showed significant increases in reports of xenophobia and religious intolerance. Brazil's political polarisation has migrated online, creating a volatile environment for digital rights.

### Organized Crime and Digital Platforms

Brazilian organised crime groups—particularly the Primeiro Comando da Capital (PCC) and Comando Vermelho (CV)—have adopted digital tools for recruitment, communication, and financial management. The use of encrypted messaging platforms, cryptocurrency, and social media for criminal coordination represents a growing enforcement challenge.

## Legal and Normative Framework

Brazil is an active participant in international cybercrime norm-setting. The Brazilian government's position on a UN cybercrime convention, submitted to the UN Ad Hoc Committee in 2022, proposed:
- Minimum criminalisation obligations for substantive cybercrime law.
- Minimum procedural obligations enabling timely investigation and prosecution.
- An international cooperation network with direct channels for evidence preservation and mutual legal assistance.

This position reflects Brazil's desire to shape a convention that balances effective law enforcement with human rights protections—an approach informed by its domestic experience with multi-stakeholder governance through CGI.br and NIC.br.

## Challenges and Tensions

### Attribution and Jurisdiction

As in most jurisdictions, attribution remains difficult. Brazilian law enforcement faces challenges in tracing cross-border cybercrime, particularly when attacks originate in jurisdictions without cooperation agreements.

### Resource Asymmetry

SaferNet's hotline processes over 2,500 reports daily, but investigative capacity does not match reporting volume. The Public Prosecutor's Office receives referrals but cannot pursue all cases. This creates a bottleneck where detection exceeds response.

### Privacy and Surveillance Tensions

Brazil's constitutional protections for privacy and free expression create tension with law enforcement demands for expanded surveillance powers. The multi-stakeholder governance model—which includes civil society in internet governance—provides a check on executive overreach, but also slows legislative response.

### The Human Rights Dimension

UNODC's engagement with Brazil highlights that cybercrime is not merely a technical or criminal justice issue. It is a human rights issue: CSAM violates children's rights; hate speech targets protected groups; online fraud disproportionately affects vulnerable populations. The Brazilian model, which treats cybercrime as a human rights violation rather than merely a property crime, is distinctive in the region.

## Regional Significance

Brazil's cybercrime architecture has implications for Latin America beyond its borders. NIC.br's technical model has been studied by other Latin American internet governance bodies. SaferNet's hotline-helpline-awareness tripartite structure has been proposed as a template for other jurisdictions. Brazil's engagement with UNODC and its position on the UN cybercrime convention positions it as a regional normative leader.

However, the model has limitations. Other Latin American states lack Brazil's institutional depth in multi-stakeholder internet governance. The Brazilian model required decades of civil society development, technical capacity-building, and political consensus that is not easily replicated.
