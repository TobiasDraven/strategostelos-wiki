# Tactical Cyber Operations — Platoon and Company Integration

> *"It's not cyber for the sake of cyber, but cyber integrated into other means."*
> — LTG Edward Cardon, Commander, U.S. Army Cyber Command, 2015

---

## Cyber at the Tactical Edge

Cyber operations are no longer confined to remote strategic facilities. The Army is pushing cyber and electromagnetic activities (CEMA) down to the platoon and company level. Expeditionary Cyber Teams (ECTs) now maneuver with tactical units. They provide offensive cyber effects, electronic warfare support, and cyber defense where the fight happens.

This page covers the integration of cyber capabilities at the platoon and company level. It focuses on how small unit commanders employ tactical cyber and coordinate with higher headquarters.

---

## The Electromagnetic Spectrum as Battlespace

### Cyberspace and EW Convergence

Cyberspace operations and electronic warfare (EW) both depend on the electromagnetic spectrum (EMS). Cyberspace operates on wired networks but also uses wireless access points. EW operates entirely within the EMS. Together, they provide commanders with cross-domain capabilities at the tactical level.

The Army integrates these through **Cyberspace Electromagnetic Activities (CEMA)** — the planning, integration, and synchronization of cyberspace and EW operations at echelons corps and below.

### Three Components of CEMA

1. **Electronic Warfare (EW).** Electronic attack (jamming), electronic protection, and electronic warfare support (direction finding, geolocation).

2. **Cyberspace Operations.** Defensive cyberspace operations (DCO), offensive cyberspace operations (OCO), and Department of Defense information network (DODIN) operations.

3. **Spectrum Management.** Synchronising friendly use of the EMS to avoid interference and maximise effectiveness.

## The Expeditionary Cyber Team

### What an ECT Does

An ECT is a scalable formation that augments maneuver units from brigade down to company level. It provides:
- **Offensive cyber effects.** Exploiting enemy networks, disrupting communications, gaining access to closed networks.
- **Electronic warfare support.** Sensing the EMS, direction finding, geolocating enemy emitters.
- **Cyber defense.** Monitoring the unit's own networks for intrusion and attack.

### How ECTs Operate

An ECT has two elements:
1. **Expeditionary element.** Offensive cyber operators that maneuver with the company. They generate effects and provide overwatch.
2. **Planning element.** Offensive and defensive planners embedded with the brigade or battalion CEMA cell. They help plan operations and coordinate effects.

During operations, the ECT operates no more than one phase line behind the forward line of own troops (FLOT). They sense, find, and report enemy emitters. They provide targeting information for both lethal and non-lethal fires.

## Platoon-Level Integration

### The Platoon Commander's Role

At platoon level, you do not plan cyber operations. You integrate cyber effects into your tactical plan. Your responsibilities include:

- **Understand the capabilities.** Know what the ECT attached to your platoon can and cannot do.
- **Provide security.** Protect the ECT operators. They are high-value, low-density assets.
- **Communicate intentions.** Tell the ECT what effect you want and when. Let them determine how to achieve it.
- **Report enemy emitters.** If you detect enemy communications or electronic emissions, report them immediately.

### Coordinating with the ECT

The ECT leader advises you on the electromagnetic environment. They can tell you:
- Where enemy emitters are located.
- What frequencies the enemy is using.
- Whether the enemy is jamming your communications.
- Whether your own emissions are exposing your position.

Use this information to plan your movement, coordinate fires, and maintain communications security.

## Company-Level Integration

### The CEMA Cell

At company level, the executive officer (XO) or a designated CEMA coordinator integrates cyber and EW into the company's operations. The CEMA cell at higher headquarters provides planning support and coordinates effects across the battalion.

### Key Tasks at Company Level

1. **Battle tracking.** Maintain awareness of the electromagnetic environment. Plot enemy emitters and report changes.

2. **Communications security.** Enforce emissions control (EMCON) procedures. Limit radio transmissions. Use wired communications when possible.

3. **Coordinate with adjacent units.** Share information about enemy electronic emissions and cyber threats.

4. **Support targeting.** Provide electronic warfare support data to the fire support team. Help identify high-value targets for both lethal and non-lethal fires.

## Planning Considerations

### METT-TC2

The Army is evolving its planning framework to include cyber as a distinct mission variable. METT-TC2 adds the second "C" for cyber. It evaluates cyberspace through three layers:
- **Physical network layer.** The hardware, cables, and wireless access points.
- **Logical network layer.** The software, protocols, and data flows.
- **Cyber-persona layer.** The user identities and access permissions.

### Cyber Key Terrain

Just as physical key terrain provides tactical advantage, cyber key terrain provides advantage in the network environment. It includes:
- Critical network nodes and servers.
- Wireless access points.
- Communication relays.
- Enemy command and control networks.

Identify cyber key terrain during mission analysis. Plan to seize, hold, or defend your own. Plan to disrupt or destroy the enemy's.
