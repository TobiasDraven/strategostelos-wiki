# Task Group Commander's Maritime Operations — Leading the Surface Action Group

> *"The essence of distributed lethality is that continuous emissions will be fatal and allow the enemy to strike first."*
> — CAPT (Ret.) Wayne P. Hughes Jr.

The task group commander leads a Surface Action Group (SAG) or similar formation. The task group is the basic tactical formation of the surface navy. It consists of multiple ships operating together under a single commander. The task group commander must coordinate the actions of multiple ships while empowering individual captains to act independently.

This page discusses the maritime domain at the task group level, relevant to commanders of destroyer squadrons, amphibious ready groups, and similar formations.

---

## The Task Group Commander's Maritime Problem

The task group commander operates in a three-dimensional battlespace. The commander must coordinate anti-surface, anti-air, and anti-submarine warfare simultaneously. The commander must balance the need for concentrated firepower against the risk of concentrated enemy attack.

### The Distributed Lethality Concept

Modern naval operations emphasise distributed lethality. Rather than concentrating all ships in a tight formation, distributed lethality spreads ships across a wide area. Each ship operates as an independent kill platform. The group achieves cumulative sea control through distributed operations.

The task group commander must trust individual captains to act on commander's intent without waiting for orders. This requires mission command — the delegation of authority to the lowest level of command.

### The Scouting Problem

The task group commander must maintain a comprehensive picture of the battlespace. This requires integrating information from multiple sources: ship sensors, aircraft, submarines, satellites, and other forces. The commander must process this information quickly and make decisions in seconds.

In emission control conditions, the task group may have to operate passively. The commander must weigh the need for information against the risk of detection.

## The Task Group Commander's Tactical Decisions

### Formation

The task group commander selects the formation based on the threat. For air defence, circular formations provide the best protection. For surface warfare, formations with width provide better scouting. For submarine warfare, formations with depth are preferred.

The task group commander must be able to shift formations quickly as the threat changes. The transition from one formation to another is a vulnerable moment.

### Weapons Employment

The task group commander coordinates weapons employment across multiple ships. The commander must prevent fratire and ensure that weapons are used effectively. The commander must decide when to employ long-range weapons and when to conserve them for close-range engagement.

### Manoeuvre

The task group commander manoeuvres the group to gain tactical advantage. Manoeuvre positions the group for scouting and shooting. It also reduces the group's vulnerability to enemy weapons. The task group commander must coordinate the movement of multiple ships while maintaining formation integrity.

---

## The Task Group Commander's Warfare Areas

### Anti-Surface Warfare

The task group commander fights surface actions using coordinated anti-ship missile attacks. The goal is to destroy the enemy's surface combatants before they can launch their own missiles. The task group commander must coordinate the timing of missile launches to achieve maximum effect.

### Anti-Air Warfare

The task group commander defends the group against air attack. Air defence is coordinated across multiple ships. The task group commander assigns sectors of responsibility to individual ships and coordinates the employment of surface-to-air missiles.

### Anti-Submarine Warfare

The task group commander coordinates anti-submarine operations across the group. The commander assigns anti-submarine helicopters to search sectors and coordinates the employment of torpedoes and depth charges.

### Amphibious Operations

The task group commander supports amphibious operations by providing fire support, air defence, and sea control. The commander must coordinate the movement of amphibious shipping and protect it from enemy attack.

---

## The Task Group Commander and Mission Command

The task group commander practises mission command. The commander provides intent and guidance but trusts individual captains to execute. This requires mutual trust and shared understanding. It also requires captains who are trained to act independently.

Mission command in the maritime domain is complicated by the vastness of the ocean and the difficulty of communication. The task group commander must be prepared to operate in emission control conditions where communication is limited or impossible.

---

## The Task Group Commander's Landward Problem

The task group commander faces a complex maritime problem. The commander must simultaneously coordinate multiple ships, maintain situational awareness in three dimensions, and respond to threats from the air, surface, and subsurface. The commander must balance the need for concentrated firepower against the risk of concentrated attack.

The task group commander who masters distributed lethality and mission command is the task group commander who wins.
