# The Indo-Pakistani Naval War of 1971 — Operation Trident

> *"Karachi burned for seven days."*
> — Admiral S.M. Nanda, Chief of Naval Staff, 1971

## Background

On 3 December 1971, Pakistan launched pre-emptive air strikes on six Indian airfields. India responded on all fronts. In the western theatre, the Indian Navy executed one of the most audacious naval operations in modern history — Operation Trident, a missile attack on Karachi, the nerve centre of the Pakistan Navy and Pakistan's primary commercial port.

## The Strategic Innovation

India had acquired eight Soviet-made Osa-I class missile boats in 1968. These 70-ton "wasps" carried four Styx surface-to-surface missiles each, capable of sinking major warships. However, they had a critical limitation — designed for coastal defence, their operational range was only 500 km, insufficient for a round trip from India to Karachi (over 1,000 km).[^1]

Admiral S.M. Nanda's solution was brilliant: **tow the boats halfway**. On the evening of 3 December, the missile boats were towed from Mumbai to Okha by survey frigates, conserving fuel for the attack and return. At Okha, the boats were released for the final run to Karachi.

## The Attack — First-Hand Account

Commander Bahadur Nariman Kavina, Commanding Officer of INS Nipat, provides this account from the bridge:

> *"We were in an arrowhead formation with Nirghat five miles on the port quarter and Veer lagging behind on the starboard quarter. We were about 40 miles from Karachi. Nirghat reported a firm contact at a distance of about 25 miles. I could see this blip on my radar and evaluated it as a warship coming towards me in view of the high rate of closing. I therefore ordered Nirghat to alter course and engage this target."*[^2]

INS Nirghat fired two missiles at PNS Khaibar, a frontline destroyer. The ship broke in two and sank with 220 officers and sailors. INS Nipat engaged MV Venus Challenger (carrying ammunition) and its escort PNS Shah Jahan, both of which sank. INS Veer sank PNS Muhafiz, an adjutant-class minesweeper. INS Nipat continued toward Karachi and fired missiles at the Kemari oil storage tanks, igniting massive fires visible from 30 miles away.[^2]

The entire operation lasted 90 minutes. Six missiles were fired. Three Pakistani naval vessels were sunk. Oil storage facilities were destroyed. No Indian vessel was damaged.

## First-Hand — The Receiving End

Commander A.D. Rao, third officer on INS Veer, recounts:

> *"We were told to write letters to our families before leaving for the mission in the dead of the night from Mumbai on December 3. Everything was top secret. Even the transportation of the boats... was done in a very clandestine manner."*[^3]

## Admiral Nanda's Gamble

Admiral Nanda had prepared meticulously. In November 1971, at a press conference in Jamnagar, he publicly declared:

> *"And if war comes again, I assure you that we shall carry it right into the enemy's biggest ports, like Karachi. I know this harbour quite well... given the opportunity, the Indian Navy will make the world's biggest bonfire of it."*[^4]

He had tested the missiles on a towed target 30 miles out to sea, watched the strike through a helicopter, and called all ships to witness — building faith in the system before the war.[^4]

## Aftermath and Lessons

Operation Trident was repeated on 8 December as Operation Python, sinking more ships and torching oil stores again. The Pakistan Navy declared a complete blockade of Karachi harbour. No ship could enter or leave.

**Lessons:**

1. **Innovation overcomes limitations**: Towing the boats transformed a coastal defence asset into an offensive strike capability.
2. **Surse and secrecy**: Total radio and radar silence, combined with towing (which eliminated engine noise signatures), achieved complete surprise.
3. **Missile warfare has arrived**: The operation demonstrated that small, fast missile boats could sink major warships — a revolution in naval warfare.
4. **Blockade without declaration**: By destroying Pakistan's oil and ammunition supplies and choking resupply routes, India achieved an effective blockade without formally declaring one.
