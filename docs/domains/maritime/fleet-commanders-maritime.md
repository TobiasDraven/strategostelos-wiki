# Fleet Commander's Maritime Operations — The Joint Force Maritime Component

> *"The numbered fleet commander integrates fleet and joint capabilities into tactical operations, employing naval power in a holistic manner across platforms and domains."*
> — NWP 3, *Fleet Warfare*

The fleet commander operates at the operational level of maritime warfare. The commander leads a numbered fleet or joint force maritime component. The fleet commander integrates surface, subsurface, air, amphibious, and information capabilities to achieve sea control and project power ashore.

This page discusses the maritime domain at the fleet level, relevant to flag officers and senior staff.

---

## The Fleet Commander's Maritime Problem

The fleet commander operates across the full spectrum of competition — from peacetime engagement to major combat operations. The commander must integrate capabilities across domains and services to achieve maritime superiority.

### Sea Control as the Primary Objective

Sea control is the Navy's foremost objective. Without sea control, no other naval function is possible. The fleet commander establishes sea control through the integration of surface, subsurface, and air forces.

Sea control is geographic and time-based. The fleet commander cannot control all the sea all the time. The commander must prioritise areas and times based on the joint force commander's objectives.

### Power Projection

The fleet commander projects power ashore through carrier air wings, cruise missiles, amphibious forces, and naval gunfire. Power projection requires sea control in the objective area. It also requires air superiority and suppression of enemy air defences.

---

## The Fleet Commander's Operational Art

### Planning

The fleet commander develops operational plans that integrate naval forces with other components. Planning includes:

- **Campaign design:** The arrangement of naval operations to achieve strategic objectives.
- **Force allocation:** The assignment of forces to tasks based on capability and availability.
- **Logistics planning:** The sustainment of forces across vast distances.
- **Risk assessment:** The identification and mitigation of operational risks.

### Preparation

The fleet commander prepares the fleet for operations. Preparation includes training, exercises, and pre-positioning of forces. The fleet commander must ensure that the fleet is ready to transition from peacetime to combat operations quickly.

### Execution

The fleet commander executes the campaign through mission-type orders. The fleet commander provides intent and guidance but trusts subordinate commanders to execute. This requires mutual trust and shared understanding.

### Assessment

The fleet commander assesses the progress of the campaign and adjusts as needed. Assessment includes battle damage assessment, intelligence updates, and logistics status.

## The Fleet Commander's Warfare Areas

### Maritime Interception Operations

The fleet commander conducts maritime interception operations to enforce sanctions, blockades, and exclusion zones. This requires the ability to visit, search, and divert vessels at sea.

### Amphibious Operations

The fleet commander supports amphibious operations by providing sea control, air superiority, fire support, and logistics. The fleet commander coordinates the movement of amphibious shipping and protects it from enemy attack.

### Strike Operations

The fleet commander conducts strike operations against land targets using carrier aircraft and cruise missiles. Strike operations require suppression of enemy air defences and precision targeting.

### Maritime Security Operations

The fleet commander conducts maritime security operations to protect shipping, ports, and critical infrastructure. This includes counter-piracy, counter-terrorism, and counter-smuggling operations.

---

## The Fleet Commander and Multi-Domain Operations

The fleet commander integrates capabilities across all domains. Space provides communications and positioning. Cyberspace provides information and electromagnetic effects. Land-based aircraft provide airpower. Land forces provide security for naval bases.

The fleet commander must be able to operate in a contested electromagnetic environment. This requires emission control, electronic warfare, and the ability to fight without network support.

---

## The Fleet Commander's Landward Problem

The fleet commander faces a complex maritime problem. The commander must simultaneously establish sea control, project power ashore, and support joint operations. The commander must integrate capabilities across domains and services. The commander must operate across vast distances with limited forces.

The fleet commander who masters operational art and multi-domain integration is the fleet commander who wins.
