# Maritime Domain — Nature and Military Relevance

> *"The United States is the most powerful nation on earth, dependent upon transoceanic links commercial and military to allies, friends, and interests."*
> — Naval Doctrine Publication 1

The maritime domain is the vast expanse of water that covers approximately 71 percent of the Earth's surface. It includes the oceans, seas, estuaries, coastal waters, and the airspace above them. It is the medium through which 90 percent of global trade flows and the arena in which naval forces have contested for centuries.

This page examines the nature of the maritime domain, its enduring characteristics, and its centrality to military operations.

---

## The Nature of the Maritime Domain

### A Global Commons

The maritime domain is the ultimate global commons. Unlike land territory, which is subject to national sovereignty, much of the ocean is international water. Ships of all nations traverse these waters in peace and war. This freedom of navigation is the foundation of global commerce and naval power projection.

The law of the sea, codified in the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), establishes the legal framework for maritime operations. Coastal states exercise sovereignty over their territorial seas (up to 12 nautical miles) and jurisdiction over their exclusive economic zones (up to 200 nautical miles). Beyond these limits lie the high seas, open to all.

### A Three-Dimensional Battlespace

The maritime domain is unique in its three-dimensional character. Operations occur simultaneously in three dimensions:

- **Up:** Air warfare and air defence above the fleet
- **Out:** Surface warfare across the sea
- **Down:** Undersea warfare beneath the surface

Each dimension presents distinct challenges. Aircraft operate at high speed but limited endurance. Surface ships provide persistent presence but are vulnerable to air and submarine attack. Submarines offer stealth and endurance but limited sensors and weapons.

### The Scouting Problem

The defining characteristic of naval combat is the vastness of the ocean and the difficulty of finding the enemy. Wayne Hughes, the dean of naval tacticians, wrote that *"the battle will be decided by scouting effectiveness and weapon range"* (Hughes, *Fleet Tactics and Naval Operations*, 2018).

In the age of sail, scouting was limited to the horizon. In the modern era, sensors extend far beyond the horizon, but so do weapons. The modern naval duel is a race between scouting and weapons. The force that finds the enemy first and strikes first generally wins.

---

## The Navy's Foremost Function: Sea Control

Sea control is the Navy's primary objective. Without sea control, no other naval function is possible. Sea control enables the movement of military and commercial shipping. It enables the projection of power ashore. It enables the blockade of an enemy's coast.

Sea control is geographic and time-based. No navy can control all the sea all the time. Sea control is established *"for just long enough, over just enough area, to achieve a desired outcome"* (JDP 0-10). It is a spectrum of activity, from local sea control in a confined waterway to oceanic command of the sea.

Sea denial is the opposite of sea control. Sea denial prevents an enemy from using the sea. It is achieved through submarines, mines, land-based anti-ship missiles, and asymmetric tactics.

## Enduring Characteristics of the Maritime Domain

### 1. The Primacy of Attrition

Naval combat is fundamentally a process of attrition. Attrition comes from the successful delivery of firepower. The force that delivers more effective firepower over time prevails. This is true whether the combatants are galleys exchanging cannon broadsides or destroyers launching anti-ship missiles.

### 2. Scouting Determines Victory

Scouting — locating the enemy sufficiently to deliver effective firepower — is the crucial process of naval tactics. The force that scouts more effectively gains the initiative and strikes first. Hughes wrote that *"the choice of tactics will also be governed by scouting effectiveness and weapons range."*

### 3. Command and Control Transforms Potential into Reality

Command and control (C2) transforms scouting and firepower potential into the reality of delivered force. The force with better C2 — better situational awareness, better decision-making, better coordination — will prevail. In the age of network-centric warfare, C2 is both a strength and a vulnerability.

### 4. Maneuver Positions Forces to Scout and Shoot

Maneuver is the activity by which C2 positions forces to scout and shoot. The force that maneuvers more effectively gains the advantage. Maneuver in the modern era is not merely geographic positioning. It is the integration of capabilities across domains — the orchestration of sensing, shooting, and countersensor/shooting capabilities across the entire fleet.

### 5. The Side That Attacks Effectively First Generally Wins

Hughes's basic precept is that naval combat is a duel, and the side that attacks effectively first generally wins. This is the temporal equivalent of the land warfare principle of seizing the initiative. The force that acts inside the enemy's decision cycle gains a cumulative advantage.

### 6. Maritime Forces Are Self-Sustaining

Maritime forces can deploy independently and provide a persistent, self-sustaining forward presence. Ships carry their own logistics — fuel, food, ammunition, and spare parts. This self-sustaining quality gives maritime forces unique strategic flexibility.

### 7. Maritime Power Enables Joint Operations

Maritime power enables joint operations. Sea control enables the movement of land forces by sea. Aircraft carriers provide airpower independent of land bases. Amphibious forces project power from sea to land. The maritime domain is the enabling medium for joint campaigns.

## Naval Warfare Areas

Naval forces fight in multiple warfare areas simultaneously:

### Surface Warfare

Surface warfare is the massing of fires to take or sink enemy ships. It has evolved from cannon broadsides to a combined-arms effort involving aircraft, ships, submarines, and shore-based systems.

### Undersea Warfare

Undersea warfare encompasses actions to establish and maintain control of the undersea portion of the maritime domain. It includes antisubmarine warfare, mine warfare, and submarine operations. Undersea warfare remains the most intricate and complex tactical problem.

### Air and Missile Defence

Air and missile defence protects the fleet from aircraft, cruise missiles, and ballistic missiles. It includes active and passive measures — fighters, surface-to-air missiles, electronic warfare, and decoys.

### Strike Warfare

Strike warfare is the use of naval forces to deliver lethal and nonlethal fires to create desired effects against targets on land. It includes aircraft strikes, cruise missile strikes, and naval gunfire support.

### Amphibious Warfare

Amphibious warfare is the projection of naval forces into, and their employment within or from, a foreign country and adjacent waters. It includes amphibious assaults, raids, and withdrawals.

### Mine Warfare

Mine warfare includes the laying of mines to deny sea areas to the enemy and the clearing of mines to enable friendly operations. Mines are the most cost-effective sea denial weapon.

### Information Warfare

Information warfare encompasses operations to exploit, deceive, degrade, or destroy enemy information and information systems. It includes electronic warfare, cyber operations, psychological operations, and deception.

---

## The Maritime Domain and Multi-Domain Operations

Modern maritime operations are inherently multi-domain. Maritime forces operate in the air, on the sea surface, under the sea, in space, and in cyberspace. They enable operations on land. They depend on space-based communications and positioning. They are vulnerable to cyber attack.

The future of maritime operations lies in distributed lethality — the integration of capabilities across widely distributed platforms. This requires tight integration of sensing, shooting, and C2 across the fleet, loosening the ties of geography and organisational boundaries.

---

## Implications for Military Operations

The maritime domain has direct implications at every level of war:

- **Strategic:** Maritime power enables global power projection. Control of the seas enables the movement of military and commercial shipping. Denial of the seas can strangle an enemy's economy.

- **Operational:** Maritime campaigns are designed to achieve sea control and project power ashore. The arrangement of naval forces to support joint campaigns is the essence of operational art at sea.

- **Tactical:** Naval engagements are fought at specific locations and times. The coordination of surface, air, and undersea forces to destroy enemy naval forces is the essence of naval tactics.
