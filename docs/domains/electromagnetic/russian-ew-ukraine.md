# Russian Electronic Warfare in Ukraine (2022–)

> *"This is a war of technologies."*
> — Colonel Ivan Pavlenko, Chief of Ukrainian General Staff's Electronic and Cyber Warfare Department, BBC, 2023

## The Expectation Gap

When Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, Western analysts expected Russian electronic warfare (EW) to be devastating. Russia had invested heavily in EW since the 2008 military reforms, with EW troops established as a separate branch of the armed forces. Over 60 different EW device models had been developed, and Russia had 18,000 EW troops at the start of the invasion.[^1]

The reality was different. Russian EW underperformed catastrophically in the initial phase. Ukrainian air defence systems remained functional. Ukrainian communications — including cellular networks — largely stayed operational. Villagers used mobile phones to tip off Ukrainian forces about Russian column movements near Mykolaiv.[^1]

## Phase 1: Failure (February–November 2022)

Several factors contributed to the initial failure:

**Electromagnetic fratricide**: Russian EW systems interfered with their own communications. Russian soldiers were often left without comms and resorted to using cell phones — which Ukrainian EW operators eavesdropped on.[^2]

**Lack of planning**: The secretive nature of invasion planning gave EW specialists little time to prepare. They lacked detailed information on Ukrainian equipment, frequencies, and operational scenarios.[^3]

**Unwieldy systems**: Russian EW systems were large, vehicle-borne platforms designed for defensive operations. They were not agile enough to keep up with advancing forces.[^1]

**Ukrainian resilience**: Having learned from 2014, Ukraine was prepared for EW attacks. The US provided SINCGARS radios that conferred resilience against Russian jamming. Starlink provided backup communications.[^3]

## Phase 2: Adaptation (November 2022–)

By November 2022, Russia had altered its tactics. The shift to attrition strategy enabled effective deployment of EW systems along stabilised frontlines.[^4]

**Key adaptations:**

- **Dispersal**: From roughly 10 EW complexes per 20 km of front to 1 major EW system every 10 km, with additional supporting assets.[^5]
- **Counter-UAS effectiveness**: Russian EW became highly effective against Ukrainian drones. The Shipovnik-Aero EW complex can take control of a drone and obtain operator coordinates with 1-meter accuracy, then relay to artillery.[^1]
- **GNSS jamming**: Systems like Zhitel and Pole-21 jam GPS within 30 km, degrading JDAM bombs, GMLRS rockets, and Excalibur shells. Excalibur accuracy dropped from over 50% to under 10% due to Russian electronic countermeasures.[^4]
- **Krasukha-4**: This strategic EW system jams airborne and air defence radars, disorients AIM-120 AMRAAM missiles, and interferes with Patriot radar stations.[^1]

## The Invisible War

Ukrainian EW officer "Alain Delon" (call-sign) describes the invisible battle:

> *"Use single rounds. This way we will be able to last till the morning [if they come closer]."*[^1]

His team detects electronic signals from Russian weapons — drones, air defence, jammers, artillery — determines the type and coordinates, then passes targeting data to strike units. Before using precision-guided munitions, they must first check for suppression in the area. If jamming is present, they must find and destroy the jammer first.[^1]

## Lessons

1. **EW is a system-of-systems**: EW cannot succeed without integration with kinetic operations. Russian EW failed initially because it was not combined with effective combined arms manoeuvre.
2. **Adaptation is continuous**: The conflict is a "move and counter-move" race. Ukrainian patches to counter Russian EW are soon countered by Russian adaptations.[^1]
3. **Quantity has a quality**: Russia deploys one large EW system every 10 km of front. Ukraine cannot match this density.[^1]
4. **Unsecured comms are fatal**: Russian use of unencrypted radios and cell phones enabled Ukrainian eavesdropping and targeting.
5. **Strategic EW is different from tactical EW**: Krasukha-4's ability to jam AWACS and Patriot radars demonstrates that EW has strategic implications beyond the tactical level.
