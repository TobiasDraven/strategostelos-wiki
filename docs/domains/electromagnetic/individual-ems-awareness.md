# Individual EMS Awareness — The Soldier's Electromagnetic Discipline

> *"Every transmission exposes a signature vulnerable to enemy exploitation. Control your emissions."*
> — SATTL, *Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations for Military Service Members*

---

## The Soldier as Emitter

Every soldier carries devices that emit electromagnetic energy. Radios, GPS receivers, smartphones, smartwatches, fitness trackers, and even Bluetooth earpieces all create detectable signatures. The enemy uses these signatures to locate, identify, and target friendly forces.

This page covers the individual discipline every soldier must practise to survive in the electromagnetic domain.

---

## Understanding the Threat

### How the Enemy Uses the EMS

Adversaries employ electronic support (ES) systems to:
- **Detect your emissions.** Every radio transmission, GPS signal, and Wi-Fi connection can be detected from kilometres away.
- **Identify your unit.** Different units use different frequencies and equipment. The enemy builds an electronic order of battle from your emissions.
- **Locate your position.** Direction-finding systems can pinpoint your transmitter's location.
- **Target your position.** Once located, your position can be struck with artillery, air attack, or electronic attack.

### Real-World Examples

- **Ukraine 2014.** Russian forces targeted Ukrainian artillery battalions using location data from soldiers' personal smartphones.
- **Modern conflicts.** Adversaries use commercially available equipment to detect and exploit friendly emissions. Cheap drones with RF sensors can locate infantry positions.

## Emission Control (EMCON)

### Principles of EMCON

Emission control is the management of electromagnetic emissions to avoid enemy detection. Key principles:

1. **Minimum power.** Use the lowest power setting necessary for communications. Higher power increases detection range.

2. **Transmission brevity.** Keep radio calls short. Use brevity codes and prowords. Long transmissions are easier to detect and locate.

3. **Directional antennas.** Use directional antennas when possible. They focus the signal toward the intended receiver and reduce exposure to enemy sensors.

4. **Terrain masking.** Position yourself behind hills or buildings to shield transmissions from enemy direction-finding systems.

5. **Vary patterns.** Change transmission times and frequencies. Predictable patterns make enemy collection easier.

6. **Lower antennas.** Reduce antenna height. Lower antennas reduce detection range while maintaining local communications.

### Personal Devices

Personal electronic devices are a major vulnerability:
- **Smartphones** continuously transmit and can reveal your location.
- **Smartwatches and fitness trackers** emit Bluetooth and Wi-Fi signals.
- **Bluetooth earpieces** create detectable signatures.

**Leave personal devices behind when deploying.** If you must carry them, disable all wireless functions and remove the battery if possible.

## Communications Security

### Radio Procedures

- **Use frequency-hopping radios** (SINCGARS) when available. They hop across frequencies at 100 hops per second, making interception and jamming difficult.
- **Use encrypted communications** when available.
- **Avoid plain-language transmissions.** Use codes and brevity lists.
- **Do not transmit unnecessary information.** Every transmission is a risk.

### When Jammed

If you experience jamming:
- **Do not increase power immediately.** This may reveal your position further.
- **Switch to an alternate frequency.**
- **Use directional antennas.**
- **Report the jamming to higher headquarters.**
- **Switch to alternate means of communication** (messengers, wire lines, visual signals).

## Navigation in GPS-Denied Environments

### The GPS Threat

GPS jamming and spoofing are common in modern conflicts. Adversaries can:
- **Jam GPS signals**, denying your ability to navigate.
- **Spoof GPS signals**, providing false position data that causes weapons to miss or forces to move to wrong locations.

### Alternative Navigation

When GPS is denied:
- **Use terrain association.** Match map contours to ground features.
- **Use dead reckoning.** Track direction and distance from a known point.
- **Use celestial navigation** (compass and stars) when possible.
- **Use inertial navigation systems** if available.
- **Pre-plan routes** with multiple navigation methods.

## Individual Protective Measures

### Before Operations

- **Remove or disable personal electronic devices.**
- **Check all equipment for proper shielding.**
- **Verify encryption settings on radios.**
- **Brief soldiers on EMCON procedures.**
- **Establish emission control conditions (EMCON levels).**

### During Operations

- **Monitor for jamming or interference.**
- **Report any unusual electromagnetic activity.**
- **Maintain emission discipline at all times.**
- **Use visual and acoustic signals when possible** to reduce radio traffic.

### After Operations

- **Report any electromagnetic incidents.**
- **Update electronic threat databases.**
- **Refine EMCON procedures based on lessons learned.**
