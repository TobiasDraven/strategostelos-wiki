# Battalion Staff EMS Planning — Integrating Electromagnetic Operations into Command

> *"The battle staff is that element within the organisation which helps the commander create and refine the battlefield image."*
> — Kahan, *Battle Staff Integration*, 1994

---

## Electromagnetic Operations at Battalion Headquarters

A battalion staff plans and integrates electromagnetic spectrum (EMS) operations through the CEMA cell and the coordinating staff. The S2 (intelligence), S3 (operations), and S6 (signal) each have distinct responsibilities for EMS operations. Together, they ensure the commander can operate freely in the electromagnetic environment while denying the same to the enemy.

This page covers how a battalion staff plans, integrates, and assesses EMS operations. It focuses on the staff processes that synchronize electronic warfare, spectrum management, and cyberspace operations with the commander's scheme of maneuver.

---

## Staff Responsibilities

### S2 (Intelligence)

The S2 advises the commander on the intelligence aspects of EMS operations:
- Conducts intelligence preparation of the battlefield (IPB) with a focus on enemy electronic warfare capabilities.
- Develops enemy electronic order of battle (enemy emitters, frequencies, locations).
- Provides threat data to support programming of unit electronic warfare systems.
- Maintains signals intelligence (SIGINT) priorities.
- Determines enemy EW vulnerabilities and high-value targets.
- Recommends guarded frequencies for the restricted frequency list.
- Assesses the effects of friendly EW operations on the enemy.
- Participates in the CEMA working group.

### S3 (Operations)

The S3 is responsible for the overall planning, coordination, and supervision of EW activities:
- Plans for and incorporates EW into operation plans and orders.
- Tasks EW activities to assigned and attached units.
- Exercises control over electronic attack, including electromagnetic deception.
- Directs electronic protection measures.
- Integrates electronic attack within the targeting process.
- Issues EW support tasks within the information collection plan.
- Coordinates with the CEMA working group to ensure EW operations support the overall plan.

### S6 (Signal)

The S6 manages the battalion's use of the electromagnetic spectrum:
- Coordinates frequency assignments for all EMS-dependent systems.
- Maintains a database of all known emitters and receivers in the area of operations.
- Coordinates with higher-echelon spectrum managers for frequency assignments.
- Manages the signal operating instructions (SOI).
- Coordinates electromagnetic interference resolution.
- Advises the commander on electronic protection measures.
- Manages the battalion's network and communications systems.

## The CEMA Working Group

### Structure

The CEMA working group is the primary coordinating body for EMS operations at battalion level. It is led by the electronic warfare officer (EWO) or the senior EW noncommissioned officer (NCO). Membership includes:
- EWO or EW NCO (leads)
- S2 representative
- S3 representative
- S6 representative
- Fire support coordinator
- Other staff as required

### Functions

The CEMA working group:
- Deconflicts friendly electromagnetic requirements with planned EW operations.
- Synchronizes EW operations with the scheme of maneuver.
- Coordinates electronic attack targeting with the fire support team.
- Resolves spectrum conflicts between units.
- Assesses the electromagnetic environment and recommends adjustments.

### Battle Rhythm

The CEMA working group meets as part of the battalion battle rhythm. During planning, it meets frequently to develop the EMS annex to the operations order. During execution, it monitors the electromagnetic environment and coordinates adjustments.

## The Military Decision-Making Process (MDMP) with EMS

### Mission Analysis

During mission analysis, the staff considers EMS-related factors:
- What are the enemy's electronic warfare capabilities?
- What friendly EMS-dependent systems are available?
- What are the terrain effects on electromagnetic propagation?
- What are the electronic threat characteristics in the area of operations?
- What constraints does the higher headquarters place on EMS use?

### Course of Action Development

The S3, with input from the S2 and S6, develops courses of action that incorporate EMS operations:
- How will EW support the main effort?
- What electronic attack capabilities are available?
- What electronic protection measures are required?
- How will friendly forces operate in a GPS-denied environment?

### Course of Action Analysis (Wargaming)

During wargaming, the staff considers EMS-related decision points:
- When will electronic attack be employed?
- How will the enemy use EW against friendly forces?
- What happens if GPS is denied?
- How will communications be maintained under jamming?

### Orders Production

The staff produces the EMS annex to the operations order. It includes:
- EW tasks to subordinate units.
- Frequency assignments and SOI information.
- Electronic protection measures.
- Emission control procedures.
- Electronic deception plans.
- Coordination instructions for EW assets.

## Spectrum Management at Battalion Level

### The Spectrum Manager

At battalion level, the S6 staff includes a spectrum manager. Responsibilities include:
- Maintaining the battalion's frequency database.
- Requesting frequencies from higher headquarters.
- Assigning frequencies to subordinate units.
- Deconflicting frequency use within the battalion.
- Coordinating with the EWO to prevent EW operations from interfering with friendly communications.
- Investigating and resolving electromagnetic interference.

### Coordination with Higher Echelon

Spectrum management is "bottom driven for requirements, top fed for resources." The battalion spectrum manager:
- Captures all spectrum requirements from subordinate units.
- Submits requirements to the brigade spectrum manager.
- Receives frequency assignments and SOI from higher headquarters.
- Distributes resources to subordinate units.

## Electronic Warfare Planning

### Electronic Support Planning

The S2, in coordination with the EWO, plans electronic support operations:
- Identifies priority intelligence requirements that can be answered by ES.
- Develops the information collection plan for ES assets.
- Coordinates with the S3 to position ES assets to support the scheme of maneuver.
- Establishes reporting procedures for ES information.

### Electronic Attack Planning

The S3, in coordination with the EWO and fire support team, plans electronic attack operations:
- Identifies high-value targets for EA.
- Coordinates EA targeting with the fire support process.
- Establishes EA control authorities and coordination procedures.
- Develops electromagnetic deception plans if required.

### Electronic Protection Planning

The S6, in coordination with the EWO, plans electronic protection measures:
- Identifies friendly systems vulnerable to enemy EW.
- Recommends technical measures (frequency hopping, directional antennas, power management).
- Recommends operational measures (EMCON, transmission brevity).
- Develops procedures for operating under jamming.

---

## Assessment

### Continuous Monitoring

The staff continuously monitors the electromagnetic environment:
- Are friendly communications functioning properly?
- Is the enemy conducting electronic attacks?
- Are there new enemy emitters in the area of operations?
- Is GPS available and reliable?
- Are EW operations achieving their intended effects?

### Measures of Effectiveness

The staff assesses EMS operations using measures such as:
- Time to detect and locate enemy emitters.
- Success rate of enemy jamming attempts.
- Effectiveness of friendly electronic protection.
- Ability to maintain communications under jamming.
- Impact of EW on enemy operations.

---

## Command Post Operations

### The CEMA Cell in the TOC

The CEMA cell operates within the battalion tactical operations center (TOC). It maintains situational awareness of the electromagnetic environment. It communicates with the brigade CEMA section. It coordinates with the scout platoon for electronic warfare support data.

### Displacement

When the TOC displaces, the CEMA cell must maintain continuity of operations:
- The S6 manages the network transition.
- The S2 maintains intelligence coverage.
- The S3 synchronizes the displacement of CEMA assets.
- Spectrum management functions continue without interruption.
