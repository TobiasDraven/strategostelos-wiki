# Tactical EMS Operations — Platoon and Company Integration

> *"The goal of tactical spectrum operations is to control the electromagnetic spectrum so that it serves the needs of friendly forces while denying use to the enemy."*
> — FM 6-02.70, *Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations*

---

## EMS at the Tactical Edge

At platoon and company level, the electromagnetic spectrum (EMS) is both an enabler and a vulnerability. You depend on it for communications, navigation, and target acquisition. The enemy depends on it for the same purposes. Whoever controls the EMS at the tactical edge gains the advantage.

This page covers the integration of EMS operations at platoon and company level. It focuses on how small unit commanders manage their electromagnetic signature, coordinate with electronic warfare assets, and operate in contested spectrum environments.

---

## The Platoon Commander's EMS Responsibilities

### Know Your Signature

Every piece of equipment in your platoon emits electromagnetic energy:
- Radios (voice and data)
- GPS receivers
- Counter-RCIED devices
- Unmanned aerial systems (UAS)
- Battle command systems
- Personal electronic devices (phones, tablets)

**Make an inventory.** Know what your platoon emits. Understand which systems are essential and which are optional. The more you emit, the easier you are to detect.

### Emission Control

As a platoon commander, you set the emission control posture for your platoon. Consider:
- **What is the threat?** If the enemy has electronic warfare capabilities, emissions must be tightly controlled.
- **What is the mission?** Some missions require continuous communications. Others require radio silence.
- **What is the higher headquarters' EMCON posture?** Ensure your platoon complies with battalion guidance.

### Coordinate with the Battalion EW Noncommissioned Officer

At battalion level, an EW NCO coordinates electronic warfare operations. At platoon level, you work with the EW NCO to:
- Request electronic warfare support (direction finding, jamming).
- Report enemy electronic emissions.
- Coordinate frequency assignments.
- Troubleshoot interference problems.

## Spectrum Management at Company Level

### The Company Executive Officer's Role

The company executive officer (XO) typically coordinates spectrum management matters. Responsibilities include:
- **Maintaining the frequency plan.** Know what frequencies are assigned to each platoon.
- **Deconflicting frequencies.** Prevent platoons from interfering with each other.
- **Reporting interference.** Document and report any electromagnetic interference to the battalion signal officer (S6).
- **Distributing the signal operating instructions (SOI).** Ensure all platoon leaders have the current SOI extract.

### The Company-Level CEMA Working Group

At company level, a formal CEMA working group may not exist. However, the commander should designate someone (often the XO or a senior NCO) to:
- Track friendly and enemy electromagnetic emissions.
- Coordinate with the battalion CEMA section.
- Advise the commander on electromagnetic protection measures.

## Electronic Warfare Integration

### What EW Can Do for You

Electronic warfare assets at brigade and battalion level can provide:
- **Electronic support (ES).** Detect and locate enemy emitters (radios, radars, cell phones).
- **Electronic attack (EA).** Jam enemy communications or radar.
- **Electronic protection (EP).** Protect your systems from enemy jamming.

### Requesting EW Support

To request EW support:
1. **Identify the need.** What enemy emitter are you targeting? What effect do you want?
2. **Submit a request.** Use the format specified in the operations order (often a spot report or a formal request through the CEMA section).
3. **Provide location information.** Give grid references and descriptions of the target area.
4. **Coordinate timing.** Specify when the effect is needed.

### Operating with Attached EW Assets

If an EW team is attached to your platoon or company:
- **Protect the team.** EW teams carry sensitive equipment and are high-value targets.
- **Provide information.** Share what you know about enemy emissions and communications.
- **Coordinate movement.** EW teams may need to move to optimal positions for sensing or jamming.
- **Respect their constraints.** EW operations require careful coordination to avoid fratricide (jamming friendly systems).

## Operations in GPS-Denied Environments

### The Threat

GPS jamming is common in modern conflicts. At platoon level, GPS denial affects:
- Navigation.
- Position reporting.
- Target acquisition (some weapons systems rely on GPS).
- Time synchronization for communications.

### Countermeasures

- **Use terrain association and dead reckoning** as primary navigation methods.
- **Pre-plan routes** with multiple navigation methods.
- **Use visual and acoustic signals** for navigation in close terrain.
- **Report GPS denial immediately** to higher headquarters.
- **Use alternative timing sources** if GPS time is lost.

## Tactical Techniques

### Patrol Base Operations

When occupying a patrol base:
- **Minimize emissions.** Use wired communications if possible.
- **Use directional antennas** to reduce signal exposure.
- **Control personal devices.** Enforce a no-personal-devices policy.
- **Maintain EMCON discipline.** Brief all soldiers on emission procedures.

### Movement

During movement:
- **Use proper radio procedures.** Keep transmissions short and infrequent.
- **Vary routes and timing.** Predictable patterns aid enemy collection.
- **Maintain communications silence** when required by the operations order.
- **Use visual signals** when radio silence is in effect.

### Attack and Defense

During offensive operations:
- **Jam enemy communications** if EW support is available.
- **Use deception.** False radio traffic can mislead the enemy about your intentions.
- **Maintain communications security.** Do not transmit sensitive information in the clear.

During defensive operations:
- **Use the EMS to detect enemy approaches.** Report enemy radio traffic.
- **Prepare for enemy jamming.** Have alternate communication plans.
- **Protect friendly emitters.** Use terrain masking and directional antennas.
