# Platoon Commander's Space-Time — Synchronising Intent Across the Battlespace

> *"The platoon leader must have a relatively complete knowledge of the terrain, and he must know as much as possible about the enemy."*
> — FM 3-21.8, *Infantry Platoon and Squad*

The platoon commander operates at the tactical level where intent is translated into action. The platoon commander receives the company commander's intent and must achieve it through the coordinated action of multiple sections. This requires mastery of space and time at a higher level than the section commander.

This page discusses the space-time domain at the platoon and company level, relevant to lieutenants and captains.

---

## The Platoon Commander's Spatial Problem

The platoon commander must visualise the battlespace in three dimensions. Where are the enemy? Where are my sections? Where is the terrain that affects the plan? Where are the boundaries between adjacent units?

### Key Spatial Tasks

The platoon commander performs several spatial tasks simultaneously:

- **Terrain analysis:** Identifying key terrain, observation and fields of fire, cover and concealment, obstacles, and avenues of approach. The platoon commander uses OCOKA and METT-TC (Mission, Enemy, Terrain, Troops, Time, Civil Considerations) to analyse the ground.

- **Section positioning:** Placing sections in positions that maximise their combat power while maintaining mutual support. The platoon commander must ensure that sections can support each other with fire and that no section is isolated.

- **Route selection:** Choosing routes that balance speed with security. The fastest route may be the most dangerous. The safest route may be too slow. The platoon commander must select the route that achieves the intent in the time available.

- **Boundary management:** Operating within the company's area of operations while coordinating with adjacent platoons. The platoon commander must be aware of boundaries to prevent fratricide and ensure seamless coordination.

---

## The Platoon Commander's Temporal Problem

Time at the platoon level is measured in minutes and hours. The platoon commander must synchronise multiple actions in time.

### Key Temporal Tasks

- **Timeline management:** The platoon commander works backwards from the time of the attack or defence. When must sections be in position? When must supporting fires begin? When must the objective be seized? The platoon commander builds a timeline that accounts for movement, preparation, and execution.

- **Synchronisation:** The platoon commander synchronises the actions of sections, supporting weapons, and adjacent units. Synchronisation ensures that all elements act at the right time to produce maximum combined effect. A section that arrives too early may be destroyed in isolation. A section that arrives too late may miss the window of opportunity.

- **Tempo control:** The platoon commander controls the tempo of the platoon's operations. Tempo is not constant. It accelerates during the assault and slows during consolidation. The platoon commander must be able to change tempo quickly in response to the situation.

---

## The OODA Loop at the Platoon Level

Boyd's OODA loop applies at the platoon level, but the cycle is more complex than at the section level. The platoon commander must process information from multiple sources: sections, the company commander, adjacent platoons, and supporting units.

### Observe

The platoon commander observes through multiple channels: reports from section commanders, radio traffic, personal observation, and information from higher headquarters. The challenge is to filter relevant information from noise.

### Orient

The platoon commander orients by matching new information with the existing understanding of the situation. Is the enemy as expected? Is the terrain as expected? Are sections where they should be? The orientation process is continuous and must be updated rapidly as new information arrives.

### Decide

The platoon commander decides on a course of action. At the platoon level, decisions are more complex than at the section level. The platoon commander must consider the impact on multiple sections, supporting weapons, and the company plan. Decisions must be communicated clearly and quickly.

### Act

The platoon commander acts by issuing orders to section commanders. The orders must be clear, concise, and timely. The platoon commander must ensure that sections understand not only what to do, but why — the intent behind the order.

---

## Synchronisation and the Platoon Sergeant

The platoon commander does not manage space and time alone. The platoon sergeant is the platoon commander's principal assistant for synchronisation. The platoon sergeant manages logistics, coordinates supporting weapons, and ensures that sections are in position on time.

The platoon commander and platoon sergeant form a two-person command team. The platoon commander focuses on the enemy and the mission. The platoon sergeant focuses on the troops and the timeline. Together, they ensure that the platoon operates as a cohesive unit in space and time.

---

## Space-Time and the Platoon Attack

The platoon attack is a space-time problem. The platoon commander must:

1. **Plan:** Analyse the terrain, the enemy, and the time available. Develop a scheme of manoeuvre that places the platoon on the objective at the right time.

2. **Prepare:** Move the platoon to the line of departure. Coordinate supporting fires. Brief section commanders. Ensure all sections understand the plan and the intent.

3. **Execute:** Control the platoon's movement to the objective. Adjust the plan as the situation changes. Commit the assault at the right time and place.

4. **Consolidate:** Secure the objective. Reorganise the platoon. Prepare for counterattack. The consolidation phase is often neglected but is critical to holding ground.

Each phase has spatial and temporal dimensions. The platoon commander must manage both simultaneously.
