# Scenario Writing

> *"A compelling scenario is paramount to the success of a wargame. If the scenario fails to engage the players, the wargame is likely to falter."*

## What Is a Scenario?

A scenario provides the context for decision-making and sets the stage for the wargame. It should include a compelling background, clear player objectives, defined relationships, available resources, and mechanisms for information updates.

## Key Elements of a Scenario

### 1. Scenario Background

A well-crafted background increases scenario credibility and deters challenges. It should:
- Establish the key actors in the wargame
- Identify their general attitudes, intentions, and goals
- Provide the political, economic, and social context
- Explain the historical and strategic background that led to the situation

### 2. Player Objectives

Clearly defining what each player is attempting to achieve creates tension or conflict between key players. Objectives should be:
- Specific and measurable
- Potentially conflicting between sides
- Aligned with the wargame's overall purpose

### 3. Defined Relationships and Command Affiliations

Establish the organizational structure or command relationship of all players:
- Hierarchy of command within teams
- Diplomatic relations between teams
- Chain of command for orders and reporting

### 4. Available Resources

Resources at each player's disposal during the wargame:
- Order of Battle (OOB) for military units
- Equipment and weapons systems
- Intelligence resources
- Logistical capabilities
- Financial and political capital

### 5. Information Update Mechanisms

How the scenario evolves and how information is shared:
- Regular situation reports (SITREPs)
- Intelligence updates
- Unexpected developments or **injects**
- Communication protocols and delays

## Designing the Scenario

### The Scenario Design Process

1. **Understand the purpose** — What analytical question is the wargame answering?
2. **Define the setting** — Where and when does the scenario take place?
3. **Establish the actors** — Who are the key players and what are their goals?
4. **Create the narrative** — What sequence of events leads to the wargame's starting point?
5. **Set the conditions** — What are the initial force dispositions, terrain, and weather?
6. **Design the injects** — What events will disrupt and challenge player plans?
7. **Validate realism** — Does the scenario reflect plausible real-world conditions?

### Scenario Robustness

A scenario should be **robust enough** to offer players latitude to make decisions about issues important to the sponsor, without being overly restrictive or permissive.

- **Overly restrictive:** Players feel railroaded; exploration is limited
- **Overly permissive:** Players lose focus; the wargame drifts from its objectives

## Narrative Quality

A compelling scenario requires a narrative that resonates with players and aligns with their real-world experiences. The narrative should:
- Be credible and internally consistent
- Reflect the operational environment accurately
- Include human elements — not just military forces, but civilian populations, NGOs, media
- Account for cultural context and the "reasonable actors" problem

## Narrative Construction

### Core Components

1. **Orientation** — Introduce the operational environment, including geography, climate, infrastructure, and human terrain.
2. **Historical Background** — Describe the strategic and operational background that explains how the situation developed. Identify the main actors, their motivations, and the conflict triggers.
3. **Escalation / Road to Crisis or War** — Chronologically unpack recent events that led to the current situation. Show a clear increase in tension and the key incidents that pushed the situation toward conflict.
4. **Mission** — State the military problem and the mission that brings the primary training audience into the situation. This may be a structured mission from higher headquarters or an unstructured problem that requires analysis and appreciation.

### Design Steps

1. **Geographical placement** — Choose real or fictional terrain. Prepare maps at appropriate scales.
2. **Identify conflict triggers** — Select one or more political, social, economic, or military triggers that start the conflict.
3. **Identify main actors** — Define each actor's aims, capabilities, power base, and threat potential.
4. **Conflict mapping** — Plot incidents on a timeline or map to control tempo and escalation.
5. **Mission statement** — Define the military mission that transitions from operational to tactical level.
6. **Supporting documents** — Prepare orders, intelligence summaries, ROE, and logistics data.
7. **Draft the narrative** — Write the full narrative, keeping it concise, accurate, and clear.

## Inject Design

Injects are unexpected developments that keep the scenario dynamic and realistic. Effective injects:
- Are timed to challenge existing plans
- Test player adaptability
- Reflect real-world uncertainty and fog of war
- Are plausible within the scenario's context

## Scenario Writing Checklist

- [ ] Clear connection to wargame objectives
- [ ] Credible background narrative
- [ ] Defined player objectives with potential conflict
- [ ] Complete order of battle for all sides
- [ ] Terrain and environmental conditions specified
- [ ] Information update mechanisms defined
- [ ] Injects planned for key decision points
- [ ] Cultural context incorporated
- [ ] Scenario validated by subject matter experts
