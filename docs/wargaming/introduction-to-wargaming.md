## Working Definition of Wargaming

Wargaming is a tool for exploring decision-making possibilities in an environment with incomplete and imperfect information *(Herman, Frost, & Kurz, 2009)*.

At its core, wargaming is a meticulous model or simulation of warfare that does not require the involvement of actual military forces. Instead, it operates independently, with the sequence of events being influenced by decisions made by players representing opposing sides. This highlights the human aspect of warfare, placing importance on the interplay between human decisions and their simulated outcomes. Consequently, each wargame is unique, making it impossible for two games to be identical.

## The demarcation: Simulation vs Wargame

The demarcation between simulation and wargaming is found in a **thinking adversary**. If there is no thinking (human) adversary or opponent that the participant must contend with, then it is a simulation. Once participants play against each other, it is termed a wargame.

In cases where both terms are appropriate, they are used interchangeably.

> **Note:** The term "wargaming" often carries negative connotations due to its association with warfare and conflict. Some perceive it as glorifying or trivializing war. However, in a military context, wargaming is a valuable tool used for strategic planning and decision-making and is not intended to lighten the realities of war.

## Critical Elements of a Wargame

A professional wargame must address five critical components:

1. **Objective** — well-defined, specific objectives that guide all game design
2. **Players** — human participants cast in operational roles reflecting their actual positions
3. **Scenario** — a compelling narrative providing context for decision-making
4. **Game Engine** — models, database, ruleset, and adjudication method
5. **Analysis / After Action Review** — structured capture of information defined by the objectives
