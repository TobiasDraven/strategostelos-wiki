# Generations of Wargames

Wargames are classified into generations based on their complexity, modelling fidelity, and underlying technology.

## First-Generation Wargames

- Abstract competition where outcomes are determined primarily by player decisions
- No sophisticated models of reality
- Turn-based; no dimension of time
- Examples: Chess, Go, Senet

## Second-Generation Wargames

- Underlying model representative of actual time and space
- Depicts combat, movement, attrition, logistics
- Accurate scaled terrain and distances
- Time incorporated — simultaneous or real-time decisions
- Examples: Kriegsspiel, Flames of War, board wargames

## Third-Generation Wargames

- Highly detailed and realistic models simulating system-vs-system interaction
- Incorporates computer systems and software
- Can simulate large-scale operations with real-world data
- Models complex interactions among various factors

**Current limitation:** No software exists that can be considered a full-fledged third-generation wargame. It is physically impossible to incorporate all possible systems and elements of power. Current "third-generation" products are second-generation wargames with additional features.

### Characteristics
- Represents entire military systems
- Examines almost all dimensions of conflict
- Focus on effects, effects-based outcomes, and synergy of systems
- Forces go beyond military to include NGOs, civilians, journalists, police

## Fourth-Generation Wargames

- Move beyond war and conflict to include **peace** as a plausible path to victory
- Blend politics, culture, religion, mass media, and global information flows with combat
- Designed for the complexities of modern warfare and fourth-generation warfare

### Characteristics
- Integration of AI, ML, and advanced technology
- Dynamic and adaptive scenarios
- Advanced analytics and feedback
- Interdisciplinary approach (politics, religion, culture, media)
- **Focus on peaceful outcomes** — peace as a desirable and plausible outcome
- Dependent on current technical trends

## The Generational Progression

```
First Gen          Second Gen           Third Gen            Fourth Gen
Abstract          Scaled Model          System-vs-System      Peace + War
Turn-based        Time + Space          Computer-assisted     AI/ML-driven
Chess             Kriegsspiel           COA wargames          Multi-domain
No fidelity       Scaled terrain        High complexity       Adaptive scenarios
Elite pursuit     Mass access           Commercial market     Global information
```
