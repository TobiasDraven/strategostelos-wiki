# SANDF Context

## Wargaming and Simulation as Critical Capabilities

A military capability is the ability to perform specific actions to achieve desired effects in the field of operation. Wargaming and simulation are capabilities in their own right — not just tools, but areas of expertise requiring specialized skills and knowledge.

They are also a **means to interrogate capabilities**. Other capabilities, such as strategic airlift, can be tested and improved through wargaming and simulation.

## DOTMLPFI Capability Framework

| Element | Simulation | Wargaming |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **D** — Doctrine and Concept | Simulation doctrine: documented framework for purpose, principles, guidelines | Wargaming concepts: methodologies, scenario development, adjudication rules |
| **O** — Organisation | Simulation units: dedicated teams for development, maintenance, execution | Wargaming cells: trained personnel within military units |
| **T** — Training | Simulation training programs: curriculum for operators, analysts, decision-makers | Wargaming workshops: scenario design and facilitation training |
| **M** — Material | Simulation hardware/software: computers, servers, modelling tools, databases | Wargaming materials: maps, counters, terrain models, physical components |
| **L** — Leadership | Simulation leadership: leaders who understand simulation capabilities | Wargaming facilitators: experienced personnel guiding wargaming sessions |
| **P** — Personnel | Simulation operators: skilled personnel operating simulation software | Wargaming participants: officers, staff, decision-makers |
| **F** — Facilities | Simulation centres: high-performance computing, networking, security | Wargaming rooms: maps, communications, collaboration spaces |
| **I** — Interoperability | Simulation integration: compatibility with C4ISR and other systems | Interoperable wargaming: testing coordination across units |

## History of Wargaming in the SANDF

- **Ancient** — Traditional African strategy games (Morabaraba, Mancala)
- **CONSIM** — Computer-assisted simulation development
- **Mission and Combat Readiness** — Training applications
- **Command Post Exercise (CPX)** — Regular use for mission and battle readiness training
- **Field Exercise (FTX)** — Live simulation
- **IMPI System** — SANDF simulation platform

## Global State

### Africa
- African Union promoting wargaming for conflict prevention and resolution
- **Kenya:** Contract with Saab for TESS; US-funded simulation facility at Isiolo
- **Nigeria:** EX LION HEART exercise using MASA SWORD and VBS4
- **African Endeavour:** Largest military communications exercise in Africa
- **AMISOM:** CPX training for AU mission officers

### BRICS
- Russia: nuclear and cyber wargaming
- India: counterterrorism and peacekeeping simulation
- China: comprehensive military simulation development

### West
- US: widespread wargaming across all domains
- EU: conflict prevention and resolution through simulation
- Commercial market: Boeing, Lockheed Martin using wargaming for market and operations

## Challenges to African Implementation

| Challenge | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| Funding constraints | Limited budgets; wargaming seen as non-essential |
| Lack of organisational will | Difficulty gaining approvals without stakeholder understanding |
| Corruption and professionalism | Undermines trust and resource allocation |
| Competition for resources | Regional disparities in wargaming capability |
| Limited capacity | Shortage of trained personnel and infrastructure |
| Complexity of African conflicts | Difficult to develop realistic wargames |
| Limited international support | Inconsistent external assistance |
| Technical and logistical challenges | Need for advanced tech and secure communications |
| Cultural and linguistic barriers | Affect design, execution, and interpretation |
| Security concerns | Protection of sensitive wargaming data |
