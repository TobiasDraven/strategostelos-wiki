# History of Wargaming

Wargaming, a manifestation of conflict in abstract form, has been an integral part of military strategy since the dawn of civilization.

## First-Generation Wargames

The earliest evidence of wargaming can be traced back to toys — miniature versions of adult tools. Young ones used these to learn how to operate in human society.

As societies evolved, games shifted towards abstraction. A miniature spear could aid in training the chieftain's son in combat, but teaching him how to strategize required a different kind of game — one that could teach leadership, decision-making, and conceptual thinking.

### Early Examples

| Game | Origin | Era | Significance |
|------|--------|-----|--------------|
| Senet / T'au | Egypt | ~2000 BC | Abstract strategic games |
| Wei-chi / Go | China | ~2300 BC | Strategic depth, elite pursuit |
| Chaturang | India | ~600 AD | Precursor to chess; spread to Persia, Middle East, Europe |
| Morabaraba | Africa | Ancient | Strategic exercise, tactical thinking for young warriors |
| Mancala | Egypt | Ancient | Strategic depth, African contribution to wargaming history |

### Characteristics of First-Generation Wargames

- **Extreme abstraction** — often impossible to determine the level of war represented
- **Symmetric forces** — game pieces are simplistic, both players have identical pieces
- **Mind-versus-mind** — focus on mental performance and decision-making
- **No underlying model** — very simplified or absent representation of reality
- **Turn-based** — participants take turns; time is ignored or abstracted
- **Elite pursuit** — wargames were primarily played by ruling or warrior elites

## Second-Generation Wargames

Second-generation wargaming was almost entirely developed in **Prussia during the 18th and 19th centuries**. The defining feature is an underlying model representative of actual time and space.

### The von Reisswitz Innovation

- **1811:** Baron Leopold von Reisswitz developed a wargame using a 3D sand table terrain model. Participants prepared orders simultaneously and provided them to an umpire for adjudication.
- **Lt Georg von Reisswitz** (the Baron's son) transformed the game by replacing the sand table with a **topographic map** — equally accurate, easier to prepare and transport. This made the game cheaper, widely accessible, and flexible across levels of command.

General Karl von Müffling, upon seeing the game, exclaimed: *"It's not a game at all, it's training for war. I shall recommend it enthusiastically to the whole army."*

### Moltke's System

General von Moltke institutionalised wargaming:

1. **Staff rides** — personally took war college students to invasion corridor terrain
2. **Map-based wargames** — conducted after staff ride discussions
3. **Live simulations / field exercises** — garrisons provided soldiers and equipment to test plans

Results were collated and often became official military plans.

### German Innovations

1. **Level-specific optimisation** — games tailored to strategic, operational, and tactical levels from the 1870s
2. **Break points** — Capt Naumann (1877) introduced rules for when units cease to function based on casualties
3. **Free Kriegsspiel** — Verdy du Vernois proposed replacing cumbersome rules with military judgment of experienced officers, enabling faster outcomes and larger-scale simulation

### Characteristics of Second-Generation Wargames

- Depicts combat, movement, attrition, and logistics
- Accurate scaled terrain and distances — break from grid-based layout
- **Time incorporated** — simultaneous or real-time decisions
- Qualifies as a **military simulation** due to model + time combination
- Focused on a single level of warfare
- Knowledge of terrain, units, own and enemy doctrine is crucial
- Intended to adjudicate the military dimension of conflict

## The Spread Beyond Prussia

Development was not restricted to the military. As literacy rates increased and elite monopoly loosened, civilian interest grew:

- **1813:** *The Peninsula* — civilian wargame with abstracted map of Portugal
- Civilian-commercial market became a major driver of innovation
