# Exercise Design

> *"Exercise design is the bridge between learning objectives and realistic decision-making. A well-designed exercise creates the conditions for commanders and staff to practice the art of command under controlled stress."*

## What Is Exercise Design?

Exercise design is the structured process of conceiving, planning, and preparing a military exercise from initial tasking through final delivery. It covers both **conceptual design** — defining the exercise concept, narrative, and objectives — and **detailed planning** — building the control structure, schedules, injects, and evaluation framework.

This page focuses on the design process itself. For scenario narrative construction, see [Scenario Writing](scenario-writing.md). For tactical decision drills, see [Tactical Decision Games](tdg/index.md).

## Exercise Types

Military exercises exist on a spectrum from study to live field execution.

| Type | Description | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Study period / staff ride** | Paper-based or terrain walk-through; low resource | Concept familiarization, doctrine review |
| **Map exercise / table-top exercise (TTX)** | Map or model-based decision-making; no troops | Command-post training, staff coordination |
| **Tactical decision exercise / TDG** | Short timed decision vignettes; individual or small group | Leader judgment drills, PME blocks |
| **Command post exercise (CPX)** | Headquarters-focused; simulated or real staff | Staff procedures, C2 systems, decision-making |
| **Field training exercise (FTX)** | Live execution with troops and equipment | Tactical proficiency, combined arms integration |
| **Computer-assisted exercise (CAX)** | Virtual, constructive, or live simulation integration | Large-scale operations, joint/combined training |
| **Command field exercise (CFX)** | Hybrid CPX + limited live execution | Command and control under semi-realistic conditions |

## The Exercise Design Cycle

### 1. Initiation and Specification

The exercise design process begins when the authority responsible for training identifies a need and issues a commissioning instruction or exercise directive. This document defines:
- Exercise name and classification
- Purpose and type
- Aim and focus
- Training and exercise objectives
- Participating and supporting forces
- Timeframe and locations
- Key appointments
- Resource allocation and constraints

### 2. Concept Development

The core planning team — usually led by an Exercise Director — reviews the commissioning instruction and develops the exercise concept. This phase should begin 8 to 12 months before execution for complex exercises.

Key outputs:
- Exercise concept paper
- Preliminary control structure
- Planning timeline
- Initial exercise objectives and training objectives

### 3. Detailed Planning

Detailed planning translates the concept into executable plans and orders. It typically involves structured conferences and work groups.

| Conference | Purpose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial Planning Conference (IPC)** | Present concept; create work groups; begin detailed planning | 4–6 months before execution |
| **Main Planning Conference (MPC)** | Integrate work group outputs; finalize exercise plan and order | 2–3 months before execution |
| **Final Planning Conference (FPC)** | Confirm readiness; review and update plan; issue final exercise order | 2–4 weeks before execution |

### 4. Execution

The exercise is executed according to the exercise order. Control is exercised through:
- Exercise control staff and observer/trainer teams
- Scheduled events and timed injects
- Real-time scenario progression and adjudication
- Safety and real-life support coordination

### 5. Post-Exercise Activities

After execution, the focus shifts to learning capture:
- Hot wash / initial after-action review
- Formal after-action report
- Lessons identified and lessons learned
- Exercise archival for future reference and reuse

## Exercise Control Structure

A balanced control structure is essential. Too large a control element strains the budget; too small a structure creates safety and control risks.

Typical core elements:
- **Exercise Director** — overall authority and final decisions
- **Exercise Control** — scenario management, timing, injects
- **Scenario Development Cell** — narrative and scenario maintenance
- **Evaluation Cell** — observation, assessment, and AAR production
- **Simulation Control** — simulation integration where applicable
- **Specialist Staff** — safety, logistics, communications, medical
- **Real-Life Support** — force protection, emergency response, administration

## Planning Fundamentals

### Backward Planning

All exercise planning should use **backward planning** from the execution date. Mark milestones for:
- Concept development completion
- Initial, main, and final planning conferences
- Mobilization and force deployment
- Rehearsals and validation events

### Objective Alignment

Every element of the exercise — from narrative to injects to evaluation — should map directly to stated training objectives. Avoid adding activities that do not serve the exercise aim.

### Narrative and Scenario Integration

The narrative provides the strategic and operational background. The scenario translates that background into the tactical situation faced by the training audience. Both must be:
- Plausible and internally consistent
- Culturally and geographically grounded
- Linked to measurable training objectives

### Inject Design

Injects are the mechanism by which the scenario remains dynamic. Good injects:
- Are timed to challenge existing plans at critical decision points
- Test adaptability, not just recall
- Reflect realistic uncertainty and fog of war
- Remain within the bounds of the exercise concept

## Exercise Documentation

Standard exercise documentation typically includes:
- Commissioning instruction or exercise directive
- Exercise concept paper
- Exercise plan and exercise order
- Narrative and scenario documents
- MEL/MIL list and control matrix
- Evaluation plan and AAR guidance
- Safety plan and real-life support annexes
