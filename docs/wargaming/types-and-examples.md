# Types and Examples of Military Simulation and Wargames

## Live, Virtual, and Constructive (LVC)

The LVC taxonomy classifies modelling, simulation, and wargames by the degree of human and system involvement.

### Live Simulation and Wargaming

Real people operate **real systems**. Involves actual forces and equipment in a controlled setting.

**Examples:**
- Field Training Exercises (FTX)
- Joint and combined-arms exercises
- Medieval jousts → modern military exercises

### Virtual Simulation and Wargaming

Real people operate **simulated systems**. Enabled by electronic simulators and computer networks. Central to these is the **Human-in-the-Loop**, which exercises:
- Motor control skills (flying a jet, tank simulator)
- Decision-making skills (committing fire control resources)
- Communication skills (C4I team member)

**Examples:**
- ARMA / Virtual Battlespace (VBS)
- Flight simulators
- Maritime simulators

### Constructive Simulation and Wargaming

Simulated people operate **simulated systems**. Real people provide inputs but do not determine outcomes. This allows examination of complex systems without actual forces or equipment.

**Examples:**
- Chess to latest campaign-planning wargames
- Command Post Exercises (CPX)

### SANDF Command Post Exercise (CPX)

The CPX is regularly used by the SANDF. Mission Readiness and Battle Readiness training are often provided through a CPX. A CPX is essentially a computer-assisted simulation of the processes and procedures that occur at or through particular levels of command and control.

## Manual / Table-Top Wargames

Executed without computers or electronic devices. Can use pen and paper.

### Formats

| Format | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Miniature Wargaming | Units represented by miniature models on model terrain |
| Board Wargames | Printed board symbolizing battlespace; forces represented by counters, miniatures, or tokens |
| Matrix Wargames | Players make structured logical arguments to advance the game |

### Matrix Wargame Example

In a scenario involving the SANDF in the DRC, a commander might argue:

> *"I shall fortify our base, and I am able to do this because:*
> - *We have a well-trained force ready to implement defensive measures.*
> - *We have an experienced engineer in the team.*
> - *The South African Government has allocated the necessary funds."*

### Advantages and Limitations of Manual Wargames

| # | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|-----------|------------|
| 1 | Accessibility — minimal resources | Time-consuming setup |
| 2 | Flexibility — easily adapted | Limited complexity |
| 3 | Tangibility — physical immersion | Human error / bias |
| 4 | No technological barrier | Oversimplification |
| 5 | Cost-effective | Lack of visual realism |
| 6 | Collaborative learning | Limited replayability |
| 7 | Develops strategic thinking | Scalability limits |

## Computer-Assisted and Digital Wargames

### Computer-Assisted Wargames

Record-keeping and calculations handled by a computer; the "meat" of the wargame still takes place in the physical world.

**Example: Virtual Advanced Squad Leader (VASL)**
- Digital rendition of Advanced Squad Leader (ASL)
- Free software; functions as a digital record-keeper
- Does not enforce rules — players control rules enforcement and decisions
- Second- to third-generation digitally-assisted wargame

### Digital Wargames

Entire wargame run digitally on a computer. The computer depicts the state of the wargame on a display, from very abstract to extremely detailed.

**Example: Hearts of Iron IV**
- Grand strategy computer simulation by Paradox Development Studio
- Player controls any country from 1936 or 1939
- Simulates ground, naval, and aerial forces
- Uses database and simplified models for interactions
- Considered a simulation (no human opponent) rather than a wargame

### Advantages and Limitations of Computer-Assisted and Digital Wargames

- Can hold much more information than human participants
- Enable large and complex wargames
- Slightly blur the lines between wargames and simulation
- Technology-dependent; require skilled operators

## Hybrid Simulation and Wargaming

Characterised by using a mixture of Constructive, Live, and/or Virtual paradigms.
