# The Wargaming Process

## Overview

The preparation, execution, and archival of a wargame broadly takes place over eight phases:

1. **Tasking**
2. **Design**
3. **Development**
4. **Testing**
5. **Rehearsal**
6. **Execution**
7. **Post-Game Analysis**
8. **Archival**

## Critical Elements

### 1. Objective

Well-defined, specific objectives are foundational. Objectives should be as specific as possible and drive the entire structure of the wargame.

- **Single objective recommended** — multiple objectives complicate design, execution, and analysis
- **Purpose types:**
  - *Experiential* — improving commanders' decision-making skills
  - *Analytical* — gathering information on decision-making processes and outcomes

### 2. Players

The human element distinguishes a wargame from a mere simulation. Players bring knowledge, experience, and decision-making abilities.

- Players should be cast in **operational roles** reflecting their actual positions
- Roles should match real-world responsibilities
- Role substitutions require additional information support (e.g., detailed OOB)

### 3. Scenario

Provides context for decision-making. A compelling scenario significantly affects the decisions players can make.

**Key elements:**
- **Scenario Background** — establishes key actors, attitudes, intentions, and goals
- **Player Objectives** — creates tension or conflict between players
- **Defined Relationships** — organizational structure and command relationships
- **Available Resources** — OOB, equipment, intelligence, logistics
- **Information Update Mechanisms** — situation reports, intelligence updates, injects
- **Compelling Narrative** — must engage players or the wargame will falter

### 4. Game Engine

Consists of models, database, ruleset, and adjudication method.

#### Models

Translate game data and player decisions into game events. Often a combination of look-up tables and mathematical expressions.

#### Database

Repository of information for player decisions:
- Available forces and capabilities
- Environmental conditions
- Technical details

#### Ruleset

Governs how and when to apply models. Sequences game events, ensures players receive the right quantity and quality of information.

Effective rulesets incorporate **fog of war** elements:
- Errors in player information
- Delays in order execution

#### Adjudication Methods

| Method | Description | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Free** | Outcome determined by facilitator/SME professional opinion | Free Kriegsspiel, matrix games |
| **Rigid** | Outcome through mathematical model or ruleset (MMT) | Rigid Kriegsspiel |
| **Semi-rigid** | Initial MMT result, modifiable by facilitator | Common in analytical wargames |
| **Consensual** | Outcome agreed by group discussion | Matrix games, seminar wargames |

### 5. Analysis / After Action Review

Ensures capture of information defined by the objectives:
- **Training game:** instructor observation and critique
- **Research game:** understanding why decisions were made
- Data collected during gameplay is the raw material for synthesis of insights

## The Eight Phases

### Phase 1 – Tasking
Initial contact, problem statements, purpose, objectives, research questions, requirements, schedule, proposal, plan of action, funding, player recruitment, game report, executive brief, authorisation.

### Phase 2 – Design
Game Design Document, Data Collection and Analysis Plan, Adjudication Plan, Catalogue Actions and Interactions.

### Phase 3 – Development
Process, players, game team augmentation, adjudication, analysis, auxiliary development, technology, communications, decision-support centre development, Mid Planning Conference, Alpha Testing, finalisation.

### Phase 4 – Testing
Feedback, Final Planning Conference, Beta Testing.
