# Role Players and Script Writing

> *"Script writing is a specialised area of scenario writing, which is used to define a specific role and behaviour that will be required of a character(s)."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 11, Appendix C

## What Are Role Players?

Role players are individuals appointed to interact with the Primary Training Audience (PTA) in a specific role to enhance the training experience. They are members of EXCON or the Response Cell who portray characters within the exercise narrative.

Role players are essential when personal interaction forms part of an event — for example, negotiations with a rebel leader, media briefings, or civilian-on-the-battlefield scenarios.

---

## Role Player Classification

Role players can be classified into the following categories:

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **OPFOR** | Portray opposing force personnel | Enemy commanders, insurgents, armed group leaders |
| **Civilians** | Portray local population | Local officials, community leaders, displaced persons |
| **Media** | Portray journalists and media personnel | News correspondents, reporters, camera operators |
| **Dignitaries** | Portray visiting officials | Government representatives, military attaches, UN officials |
| **Hostage Takers** | Portray individuals holding hostages | Terrorists, criminals, insurgents |
| **Prisoners** | Portray captured personnel | Detainees, POWs, suspects |
| **Casualties** | Portray wounded or killed personnel | Simulated casualties for medical training |

---

## Script Writing

A **script** is a document that defines the specific role and behaviour required of a character. It guides role players in terms of:

- **Portrayal style:** How the character should act (hostile, disruptive, combative, friendly, evasive)
- **Key messages:** What the character should communicate
- **Boundaries:** What the character should and should not say or do
- **Triggers:** How the character should respond to specific PTA actions

### Script Components

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Character Profile** | Name, background, personality traits, motivations |
| **Situation Brief** | Current situation from the character's perspective |
| **Key Messages** | Core information the character must convey |
| **Do's and Don'ts** | Permitted and prohibited actions/responses |
| **Contingency Triggers** | How to respond to specific PTA actions or questions |
| **Safety Considerations** | Safety boundaries and emergency procedures |

---

## Constructing a Role Player

When constructing a role player, the following aspects must be considered:

### Character Development

- **Name and identity:** A distinct name and background for the character
- **Personal details:** Age, background, personality traits (limited to what is necessary)
- **Military/past experience:** Relevant background that informs the character's behaviour
- **Motivation:** Why the character is acting as they are
- **Approach to the situation:** The character's attitude and likely responses

### Interaction Guidelines

- **Questions to expect:** What the PTA is likely to ask
- **Answers to provide:** What information the character should reveal
- **Answers to withhold:** What information the character should not reveal
- **Escalation triggers:** What PTA actions should provoke a response
- **De-escalation triggers:** What PTA actions should calm the situation

---

## Safety Considerations

All role players must be briefed on:

- **Safety boundaries:** Physical and behavioural limits
- **Emergency signals:** Code words or signals to stop the interaction
- **Prohibited actions:** What role players must never do (use real violence, reveal classified information, etc.)
- **Welfare arrangements:** Rest periods, hydration, medical support
