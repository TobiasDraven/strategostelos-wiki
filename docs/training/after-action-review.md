# After Action Review and Lessons Learned

> *"An After Action Review (AAR) is a guided analysis of an organization's performance, conducted at appropriate times during and at the conclusion of a training event, with the objective of improving future performance."*
> — US Army TC 7-0.1, *After Action Reviews*

## What Is an After Action Review?

An **After Action Review (AAR)** is a professional discussion of a training event that enables participants to discover for themselves what happened and develop a strategy for improving performance. It is **not** a critique — it does not judge success or failure. It is a structured conversation focused on learning.

**Key characteristics:**
- Conducted during and after the exercise (not just at the end)
- Led by a facilitator (controller, evaluator, or leader)
- Focuses on training objectives and standards
- Involves all participants
- Emphasises self-discovery over instruction

---

## The Four-Step AAR Process

### Step 1: Plan

Planning for the AAR begins before the exercise. The AAR plan identifies:
- What events and tasks will be observed
- Who will observe them
- When and where AARs will be conducted
- What standards will be used for evaluation

### Step 2: Prepare

Preparation begins before the exercise and continues during execution:
- Brief observers and controllers on the AAR plan
- Collect observations from working papers
- Review the sequence of events and cause-effect relationships
- Develop a discussion outline
- Prepare thought-provoking questions

### Step 3: Conduct

Each AAR contains three major steps:

1. **Restatement of training standards** — The facilitator briefly restates the specific standards
2. **Discussion of events** — The facilitator guides a discussion of what happened and why
3. **Summary and recommendations** — The facilitator summarises and identifies improvements

**Conduct techniques:**
- Ask leading, open-ended questions focused on training standards
- Have participants describe what happened in their own words
- Relate tactical events to subsequent results
- Explore alternative courses of action
- Avoid detailed examination of events not related to major training standards

### Step 4: Follow Up

- Retrain immediately to address weak task proficiencies
- Revise SOPs to reflect lessons learned
- Share lessons with other units
- Record AAR results for future reference

## Types of AARs

| Type | When Conducted | Scope |
|------|---------------|-------|
| **Formal** | Company level and above; externally evaluated events | Detailed planning, scheduled, facilitated |
| **Informal** | Crew, squad, or platoon level; on-the-spot | Minimal planning, leader-facilitated |
| **Interim** | At logical breakpoints during the exercise | Correct deficiencies before they compound |
| **Hot Wash** | Immediately after the exercise concludes | Initial impressions while fresh |

---

## The After Action Report

The **After Action Report (AAR)** is the formal written record of the exercise. It is produced concurrently with or immediately after the exercise.

**AAR Format:**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Executive Summary** | Significant lessons learned, readiness discussion, recommendations |
| **Exercise Objective** | Restated from the Commissioning Instruction |
| **General Discussion** | Significant events, lessons learned, support requirements |
| **Findings and Conclusions** | Associated with each lesson learned and deficiency |
| **Recommendations** | Specific recommendations for future training |

**Critical rule:** Formal AARs should be submitted within 1–2 weeks of exercise completion.

---

## Lessons Learned Process

The lessons learned process extends beyond the AAR to ensure observations are captured, validated, and integrated into future training:

| Phase | Activity |
|-------|----------|
| **Discovery** | Collect observations from AARs, controller reports, working papers |
| **Validation** | Screen observations to determine if a best practice or issue exists |
| **Integration** | Resolve validated issues through changes to doctrine, training, SOPs |
| **Assessment** | Observe implemented solutions to determine if they met requirements |

**Critical principle:** Lessons learned must be recorded, shared, and institutionalised — not merely identified.

---

## Conducting Effective AARs

**The facilitator's role:**
- Guide the discussion, do not lecture
- Ask open-ended, thought-provoking questions
- Emphasise the positive while addressing weaknesses
- Keep the discussion focused on training standards
- Ensure all participants contribute

**Common pitfalls:**
- Critiquing rather than facilitating
- Lecturing participants on what went wrong
- Focusing on individuals rather than processes
- Allowing the discussion to drift from training objectives
- Failing to identify actionable recommendations
- Not following up with retraining
