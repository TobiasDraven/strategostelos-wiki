# Exercise Concept Development

> *"Exercise design is the intellectual core of the exercise planning process. It is the point at which training objectives are translated into operational reality."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 7

## What Is the Exercise Concept?

The **Exercise Concept** is the blueprint for the exercise. It integrates the Main Events List (MEL) and Master Incident List (MIL) into a coherent operational narrative, with timelines, locations, safety considerations, and control arrangements. Once approved by the Exercise Director (EXDIR), the Exercise Concept becomes the framework for all detailed planning.

---

## The Three-Phase Design Process

Stage 2 — Design and Development — is itself a three-phase process:

```
STAGE 2: DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT
├── Phase 1: Objective Analysis → Final Integrated Objective List (FIOL)
├── Phase 2: Design and Concept Development → MEL, MIL, Exercise Concept
└── Phase 3: Exercise Construction → Injects, ECM, ICS
```

---

## Phase 1: Objective Analysis

The purpose of Phase 1 is to produce the **Final Integrated Objective List (FIOL)** — a validated, prioritised, and SMART-I compliant set of training objectives that will guide all design decisions.

**Key activities:**
1. **Objective Review and Reformulation** — Review initial objectives; reformulate ambiguous or overlapping ones
2. **SMART-I Compliance Test** — Ensure each objective is Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-bound, and Inspectable
3. **Grouping** — Cluster objectives into thematic groupings reflecting operational functions
4. **Integration** — Resolve conflicts and overlaps between objectives
5. **Prioritisation** — Rank objectives by criticality

**Output:** The FIOL — the single most important document in exercise design. Every subsequent product is derived from it.

## Phase 2: Design and Concept Development

### Step 1: The Main Events List (MEL)

The MEL is the structural skeleton of the exercise. It translates the FIOL's thematic groupings into a sequence of operational events.

**Construction method:**

| Step | Activity |
|------|----------|
| **1.1** | Identify Themes — Review FIOL and identify common operational themes |
| **1.2** | Sequence Themes — Order themes in realistic operational sequence |
| **1.3** | Decompose Themes into Events — Identify specific events that exercise associated objectives |
| **1.4** | Link Events to Objectives — Specify which FIOL objective(s) each event addresses |

**MEL Format:**

| Theme | Event | Objective(s) / Comments |
|-------|-------|-------------------------|
| 1. ISR | 1.1 Route reconnaissance | Obj 2: Conduct tactical reconnaissance |
| 1. ISR | 1.2 Intelligence preparation | Obj 3: Produce IPB |
| 2. Planning | 2.1 Staff estimate | Obj 4: Conduct operational estimate |

**Quality criteria:**
- Addresses every objective in the FIOL at least once
- Contains no events that cannot be traced to an objective
- Is sequenced in a logically coherent operational flow
- Is realistic given available time, space, and forces

---

### Step 2: The Master Incident List (MIL)

The MIL deconstructs each MEL event into the specific tactical incidents that will require a response from the PTA. If the MEL is the skeleton, the MIL is the nervous system.

**Construction method:**

| Step | Activity |
|------|----------|
| **2.1** | Deconstruct Each Event — Ask: "What specific occurrences will challenge the PTA?" |
| **2.2** | Define Each Incident — Define occurrence + required PTA response |
| **2.3** | Number Incidents — Number to correspond with parent event (1.1.1, 1.1.2, etc.) |

**MIL Format:**

| Event | Incident | Occurrence | Response |
|-------|----------|------------|----------|
| 1.1 Route recc | 1.1.1 | Locals report insurgent infiltration route | PTA conducts ambush |
| 1.1 Route recc | 1.1.2 | IED discovered on primary route | PTA conducts route clearance |

**Quality criteria:**
- Deconstructs every MEL event into at least one incident
- Contains no incidents that cannot be traced to an event and objective
- Provides incidents that collectively stress every objective in the FIOL
- Is realistic given the operational context and PTA capabilities

---

### Step 3: Conceptualisation and Exercise Concept Construction

Conceptualisation is not a single act; it is an iterative process of review, sequencing, mapping, safety assessment, and scheduling.

**The Conceptualisation Process:**

| Step | Activity |
|------|----------|
| **3.1** | Review Known Facts and Requirements — Type, aim, objectives, budget, locations, time, PTA, forces, control approach, exercise model, MEL/MIL |
| **3.2** | Construct the Activity List — Sequence all events and incidents in operational time |
| **3.3** | Map Placement — Plot all incidents on a training area map |
| **3.4** | Safety Assessment — Identify live-fire areas, civilian presence, evacuation routes |
| **3.5** | Scheduling — Allocate timings and plot on a Gantt chart or timeline |

**Activity List format:**

| Event / Incident | Date | Time | Location | Target Audience | Expected Outcome | Comments |
|------------------|------|------|----------|-----------------|------------------|----------|
| 1.1.1 | D+1 | 0600–0700 | Grid ABC123 | Recce Platoon | Ambush executed | Coordinate with safety |
| 1.1.2 | D+1 | 0800–0900 | Route BLUE | Recce Platoon | Route cleared | IED script attached |

## Site Reconnaissance

Once the concept is drafted, a physical site reconnaissance confirms that the training area can support the planned events.

**Reconnaissance Team:**

| Role | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Chief Real-Life Support / Exercise Warrant Officer | Accommodation, facilities, sustainment |
| Chief Scenario Development | Terrain suitability for planned incidents |
| Chief Exercise Control | Control positions, communications, observation |
| Battlefield Preparation / Simulation Representative | Live-fire areas, simulation infrastructure |
| Signal Officer | Communications footprint, cell coverage |
| Medical Officer | Medical facility access, evacuation routes |

**Focus areas:**
- **Terrain:** Size, geography, vegetation, hydrography, time/distance between locations
- **Infrastructure:** Office space, mess, ablutions, accommodation, power, water, sewerage
- **Restrictions:** Grave sites, heritage sites, environmental sensitivities, legislation
- **Safety:** Live-fire areas, civilian presence, emergency services availability
- **Liaison:** Military controlling authority, local government, security structures

**Back brief:** The reconnaissance team presents site suitability assessment, real-life support implications, and required adjustments to the EXDIR.

## Presenting the Exercise Concept

The Exercise Concept can be presented using any combination of:
- Map overlays showing event locations and timelines
- D-Day timeline showing the sequence of events
- Lines of operations showing the operational flow
- Visual mind maps or infographic showing thematic relationships
- Activity List / descriptive work document

**Presentation to the EXDIR must include:**
- Conceptual approach and broad themes
- Description of each event and supporting incident
- Sequence and timing of events
- Location(s) on a training area map
- Primary Training Audience and objectives to be achieved
- Focus areas for evaluation
- Strengths, weaknesses, risks, and implications

**EXDIR decision:** The EXDIR assesses the concept and either approves it or provides additional guidelines for adjustment.
