# Exercise Delivery and Execution

> *"Execution is Stage 4 of the Exercise Design process — the pinnacle of the exercise's design process and the 'long pole' in achieving the objectives."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 14

## Overview

**Delivery** (also called **Execution**) is Stage 4 of the Exercise Design process. At this point, all planning has been completed, the Exercise Order and Exercise Plan have been issued, and all elements are in the training area ready to start.

As with real-time operations, the start of an exercise is linked to a specific **H-hour** on a predetermined **D-day**.

---

## Key Documents

| Document | Description | Distribution |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Exercise Order** | Issued at the end of the Main Planning Conference. Includes all Support Plans. Instruction for elements identified to participate. | All role players: participants, EXCON, supporting elements |
| **Exercise Plan** | Prepared by EXCON. Sets out the sequence of events according to the approved Exercise Time Line. Detailed activity list of all events. | **EXCON Eyes Only** — never shared with the training audience |
| **Exercise Control Matrix** | Management tool used by EXCON to plan, monitor, and control delivery. Links activities to injects, locations, and date-time groups. | EXCON |
| **Schedule of Events** | List of planned events and activities **outside** the player bubble (opening/closing ceremony, DV Day, etc.) | All participants |

---

## Delivery Phases

### Phase 1: Off-Site Planning, Preparation, and Movement

Starts after the Final Planning Conference and Exercise Order distribution.

- **Off-site planning:** Each unit conducts planning and preparation at home station
- **Preparation:** Preparatory/refresher training, collecting equipment, preparing vehicles/ships/aircraft
- **Movement:** All elements move to the exercise area according to the Movement Plan

**MOVECON:** EXCON establishes a movement control cell to coordinate all movement, handle incidents, coordinate military police escorts, and manage personnel/equipment at ports of entry.

### Phase 2: On-Site Preparation, Planning, Training, and Set-Up

Activities before the exercise starts:

- **Reception and Accreditation:** Clearing in of members, accreditation, allocation of messing/workspace
- **EXCON Training:** Specialised training and orientation for EXCON staff
- **Refresher Training (PTA):** Specific drills and actions for participants
- **Response Cell Training:** OPFOR and role players train and rehearse
- **Battlefield Preparation:** Creating enemy positions, setting up targets, preparing simulated scenes
- **All Participants Brief:** Chief EXCON briefs all commanders on aim, objectives, concept, timeline, ROE, safety
- **Set-up of HQs:** Establishing headquarters and command posts
- **Opening Ceremony:** Official opening of the exercise
- **Final Rehearsal:** COMEX (communications test) and MEDEX (medical exercise)

### Phase 3: Execution (STARTEX to ENDEX/FINEX)

The exercise is initiated with the code word **"STARTEX"** on all command channels, followed by the first inject.

---

## Starting the Exercise

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Cold Start / Slow Start** | PTA gradually introduced to the situation. Opening Scenario, Warning Order, INTSUMs, INREPs, Operational Directive. |
| **Hot Start** | PTA immediately exposed to a crisis requiring urgent action. No lead-up or unfolding of events. |

Lessons learned from past exercises show that the best approach is often a **combination** of both methods.

---

## The Player Bubble

The execution of the exercise takes place in the **"Player Bubble"** — the world described in the Narrative and Scenario.

- EXCON is responsible for activating incidents within the player bubble using injects
- The A&E cell observes and measures the PTA response
- EXCON isolates the PTA from real-world and external influences to allow focus on scheduled events

---

## The Role of EXCON During Execution

### Coordination

The Chief of Staff drafts a **Meeting and Staff Liaison Plan** at the end of the MPC. Key meetings include:

| Meeting | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **EXCON Planning Meeting** | Chaired by EXDIR. Plans and coordinates activities for the next 48 hours. Attended by all EXCON cell chiefs. |
| **EXCON End of Day Meeting** | Feedback from cell chiefs on PTA performance and exercise progress. Identifies adjustments needed. |

### Control

Control requires a hands-on approach. Chief Exercise Control and control staff carry out:

- Managing the Exercise Order/Exercise Plan
- Overseeing MEL-MIL (MESL)
- Maintaining the Schedule of Events
- Managing the Exercise Control Matrix and Exercise Time Line

---

## Exercise Control Centre (ECC)

The ECC functions as the hub from where all activities are controlled and managed:

- Managing and reporting all real-time incidents
- Coordinating all safety issues
- Maintaining the war diary
- Tracking completion of events and receipt of reports
- Pausing, stopping, and restarting the exercise as instructed
- Coordinating day-to-day administration

---

## Incident Development

It is not always possible to develop all incidents and injects required during the exercise in advance. Exercise conditions may change, or the flow may be adapted. The Scenario Cell must be prepared to add additional incidents and supporting injects on an ad hoc basis.

---

## Ending the Exercise

The code word **"ENDEX"** (End of Exercise) or **"FINEX"** is transmitted over all active radio frequencies, signalling the end of the exercise. All exercise/training activities must be brought to an end.
