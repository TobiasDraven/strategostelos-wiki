# Post-Exercise Activities and Reporting

> *"Post-exercise activities complete the training cycle and serve as the starting point for the next training exercise."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 15

## Overview

Post-exercise activities are divided into two categories: **administrative tasks** and **demobilisation**. Administrative tasks focus on the writing and handing in of key reports, debriefs, and observations. Demobilisation focuses on the physical process of closing down the exercise and preparing forces to withdraw and return to their home units.

---

## In-Exercise Activities

These activities take place continuously during the exercise, directly after the completion of a specific event or activity.

### Hot Wash-Up/Debrief

A post-activity debriefing designed to identify and discuss performance as observed and experienced by participants and controlling staff.

**Key questions:**
- What was the objective aim of the activity/exercise?
- What did we (PTA) do?
- What did we (PTA) do right?
- What did we (PTA) do wrong?
- How can we (PTA) improve?

The hot wash-up is facilitated by a member of EXCON (senior controller/evaluator/observer present) and should take place as soon as possible after the completion of the training activity.

### Evaluation/Assessment Reports

Reports compiled by appointed evaluation staff reflecting the outcome of formal evaluation or assessment of the PTA. Based on the Evaluation Plan developed during the Main Planning Conference.

### Observation Reports

Compiled by controlling staff and observers within 24 hours of the completion of an event or activity. Forwarded to Chief EXCON via the Exercise Control Centre. Provides the EXDIR with accurate feedback to allow mid-exercise adjustments.

## Post-Exercise Activities

### Post Exercise Reports (PXR)

PXR are normally required only for larger exercises involving personnel and resources outside of the EXDIR's normal span of command.

#### First Impressions Report (FIR)

Compiled by all elements that participated in the planning, development, and delivery of the exercise. Completed and handed in within **48 hours** of completion.

**Layout:**
- Identification of elements or units providing feedback
- Identification of the item or topic to be discussed
- Observations made (brief description/discussion of the issue)
- Recommendations

All elements must draft a FIR to allow for a **360° reflection**. No element may withdraw if their FIR has not been presented or submitted.

#### After Action Review (AAR)

A specific date is identified for the AAR, normally **14 to 28 days** after completion. The AAR reviews the FIR to identify valid points for inclusion in the final Exercise Debrief Report.

**Focus areas:**
- Shortcomings in training to improve curriculum and method
- Shortcomings in doctrine and TTPs
- Personnel using incorrect doctrine or procedures
- Observations meriting further attention

#### Exercise Debrief Report

The **final report** for the exercise, submitted by the EXDIR to the Officer Scheduling the Exercise (OSE). Compiled from various "in exercise" report and inputs extrapolated from the FIR.

**Contents:**
- Sequence of all activities that took place during the exercise
- Exercise objectives
- Lessons learned
- Recommendations to improve the standard of the next exercise
- Reflection of participant performance

#### Lessons Identified/Learned

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Lessons Identified** | Recommendations based on observations relating to the discrepancy between required and actual performance. Not yet formally accepted or captured as doctrine/TTPs. |
| **Lessons Learned** | A Lesson Identified that has been validated and integrated into doctrine and TTPs, resulting in a verifiable improvement in military effectiveness. |

> **Critical distinction:** Lessons identified only become lessons learned once they have been integrated into doctrine and applied.

#### Rectification Plan (Follow-Up)

Based on the findings and recommendations in the Final Debrief Report, the OSE identifies and drafts a Rectification Plan. The plan indicates:

- The area of under-performance, non-performance, or concern
- The required output or performance
- The person or entity responsible for the rectification
- The date by which the rectification should be completed
- Method of reporting and to whom the report should be addressed

## Demobilisation

Demobilisation is an "operation" on its own requiring detailed planning. EXCON is responsible to centrally plan, coordinate, and control the process.

### Closing Ceremony

An official closing ceremony is held to thank and greet all exercise participants. Exercise trophies and certificates for outstanding performances and conduct may be awarded.

### Movement

On completion of the demobilisation process and closing ceremony, movement back to home units is controlled and coordinated by MOVECON, which remains in place until all units have arrived at their home units.
