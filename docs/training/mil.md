# The Master Incident List — MIL

> *"The MIL deconstructs each MEL event into the specific tactical incidents that will require a response from the PTA. If the MEL is the skeleton, the MIL is the nervous system."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 7

## What Is the MIL?

The **Master Incident List (MIL)** is the detailed decomposition of the MEL. It takes each MEL event and breaks it down into the specific tactical incidents that will challenge the PTA and force them to demonstrate the required competencies.

The MIL is the link between the structural skeleton (MEL) and the executable products (injects, ECM, ICS). Every inject, every control action, and every assessment traces back to an incident in the MIL.

---

## Construction Method

The MIL is constructed in three sequential steps:

### Step 1: Deconstruct Each Event

For each event in the MEL, ask: *"What specific occurrences will challenge the PTA and force them to demonstrate the required competency?"*

This is a creative, analytical process. The designer must think from the perspective of the PTA and identify the specific moments that will test their ability to achieve the objective.

### Step 2: Define Each Incident

An incident must contain two elements:

1. **Occurrence:** What is happening, has happened, or will happen. This creates the initiating point for PTA action.
2. **Response:** The required PTA action to address, solve, prevent, or mitigate the occurrence.

**Incident definition:** A specific, single-topic occurrence that is pre-determined and pre-planned. It is defined in terms of time and location and directed at a specified training audience. The aim of an incident is to provoke a response from the training audience, this response will then be measured against a pre-determined minimum performance standard to determine whether the objective has been met. *(Adapted from NATO AJP-3, Annex C)*

### Step 3: Number Incidents

Number incidents to correspond with their parent event (1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.2.1...). This numbering system becomes the reference framework for injects, the Exercise Control Matrix, and assessment sheets.

---

## MIL Format

| Event | Incident | Occurrence | Response |
|-------|----------|------------|----------|
| 1.1 Route reconnaissance | 1.1.1 | Locals report insurgent infiltration route at Grid ABC123 | PTA conducts ambush to neutralise insurgents |
| 1.1 Route reconnaissance | 1.1.2 | IED discovered on primary route at 0800B | PTA conducts route clearance and adjusts movement plan |
| 1.2 Intelligence preparation | 1.2.1 | UAV imagery shows enemy positions at Grid DEF456 | PTA produces updated IPB |
| 2.1 Staff estimate | 2.1.1 | Higher HQ issues warning order for offensive operation | PTA conducts operational estimate |

**Formatting rules:**
- Incident descriptions at this stage are broad. Detailed formulation occurs during Exercise Construction (Phase 3).
- Each incident must have both occurrence and response.
- Incidents are numbered to maintain traceability to MEL events and FIOL objectives.

---

## Quality Criteria

A well-constructed MIL:

- ✅ Deconstructs every MEL event into at least one incident
- ✅ Contains no incidents that cannot be traced to an event and objective
- ✅ Provides incidents that collectively stress every objective in the FIOL
- ✅ Is realistic given the operational context and PTA capabilities

## Relationship to Other Documents

```
FIOL → MEL → MIL → Injects → ECM → ICS
```

The MIL is the critical link in this chain. It translates structural events into actionable incidents that can be executed, controlled, and assessed.
