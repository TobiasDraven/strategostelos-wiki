# The Exercise Planning Process

> *"Exercise planning is a continuous process that involves many complex activities that interact, often in indirect ways. The process consists of several integrated planning conferences or sessions, staff liaison, site visits, and back briefs."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 2

## The Five-Stage Lifecycle

The exercise planning process consists of five integrated stages. Each stage builds on the previous one, and the quality of each stage determines the quality of the exercise that follows.

```
STAGE 1: COMMISSIONING
    │
    ▼
STAGE 2: DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT (Initial Planning)
    │
    ▼
STAGE 3: DETAILED PLANNING
    │
    ▼
STAGE 4: EXECUTION
    │
    ▼
STAGE 5: POST-EXERCISE ACTIVITIES
```

### Stage 1: Commissioning

The exercise is authorised and resourced. The Officer Scheduling the Exercise (OSE) defines the broad requirements, objectives, participating forces, primary training audience, and budget. The Commissioning Instruction (or Exercise Directive for joint/multi-national exercises) is issued, appointing the Exercise Director (EXDIR) and providing the framework within which the exercise will be designed.

### Stage 2: Design and Development (Initial Planning)

The creative and analytical heart of exercise planning. The design team translates objectives into an operational concept. This stage produces the **Exercise Concept**, which becomes the guiding document for all detailed planning. It culminates in the Concept Development Conference (CDC).

### Stage 3: Detailed Planning

Logistics, force generation, sustainment, and detailed planning products are produced. Work groups develop injects, the Exercise Control Matrix (ECM), Inject Control Sheets (ICS), and the final Exercise Plan. This stage culminates in the Final Planning Conference (FPC).

### Stage 4: Execution

The exercise is conducted. EXCON controls, directs, and monitors events. The PTA is assessed against the exercise objectives.

### Stage 5: Post-Exercise Activities

Hot-wash-ups (intermediate debriefs), After Action Reports (AAR), lessons learned, and knowledge management.

## Planning Conferences and Meetings

A series of conferences and meetings facilitate the planning process, each with a specific purpose and output:

### Exercise Specification Meeting

**Purpose:** Initiate the planning process and define broad requirements.

**Initiated by:** OSE

**Attendees:** EXDIR and senior EXCON members

**Key activities:**
- Define required end state, aim, and objectives
- Identify participating forces and PTA
- Establish budget and resource envelope
- Provide guidelines for Commissioning Instruction

**Output:** Commissioning Instruction / Exercise Directive

---

### Concept Development Conference (CDC)

**Purpose:** Design and develop the Exercise Concept.

**Initiated by:** EXDIR

**Attendees:** Core EXCON members

**Duration:** Days to weeks (8–12 months before execution)

**Key activities:**
- Review Commissioning Instruction and objectives
- Review lessons learned from previous exercises
- Analyse training audience and supporting elements
- Design and construct the Exercise Concept

**Deliverables:**
- Planning timeline
- Exercise timeline
- Schedule of events
- Exercise Concept document
- Preliminary EXCON structure
- Identification of work groups
- Training package (narrative, MEL, MIL, injects, assessment concept)

**Critical principle:** The CDC must be completed before detailed planning begins. Premature transition to Stage 3 risks disjointed planning.

---

### Initial Planning Conference (IPC)

**Purpose:** Present the Exercise Concept and initiate detailed planning.

**Initiated by:** EXDIR

**Attendees:** All EXCON members and selected PTA representatives

**Key activities:**
- Present Exercise Specification and Exercise Concept
- Establish decentralised work groups
- Assign detailed planning tasks

**Output:** Approved Exercise Concept; work group assignments

---

### Main Planning Conference (MPC)

**Purpose:** Integrate work group outputs into the Exercise Plan.

**Initiated by:** EXDIR

**Attendees:** All EXCON members

**Key activities:**
- Review and integrate support plans from work groups
- Finalise the Exercise Plan
- Convert the Exercise Plan into the Exercise Order

**Output:** Final Exercise Plan; Exercise Order (draft)

---

### Final Planning Conference (FPC)

**Purpose:** Confirm all planning goals have been achieved.

**Initiated by:** EXDIR

**Attendees:** All EXCON members

**Key activities:**
- Review and update the Exercise Plan
- Address any unforeseen incidents or changes
- Sign and issue the Exercise Order

**Output:** Signed Exercise Order issued to all role players

---

## Detailed Planning Phase

Once the Exercise Concept is approved, detailed planning proceeds through decentralised work groups, each focusing on key areas:

| Work Group | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Scenario / Narrative | Narrative development, MEL, MIL |
| Injects | Inject design and list development |
| Assessment / Evaluation | Training and Evaluation Plan |
| Exercise Control | ECM, ICS development |
| Real-Life Support | Logistics, accommodation, sustainment |
| Simulation | Simulation systems and technical support |
| Medical | Medical support and evacuation planning |
| Signal / Comms | Communications infrastructure |

---

## Planning Timeline

A backward planning (reverse engineering) approach is used:

1. Start from the exercise execution date (D-Day)
2. Mark key milestones backward: FPC, MPC, IPC, CDC
3. Include mobilisation periods for participating forces
4. Ensure the Exercise Concept is completed before the IPC

**Critical principle:** Initial planning should be done as early as possible and in as much detail as possible. The complexity of physical planning should not be underestimated.

---

## The Exercise Warning Order

Once the CDC is completed, the EXDIR issues an Exercise Warning Order to all stakeholders involved in detailed planning. The Warning Order indicates:
- Date, time, and place of the IPC
- Who is to attend
- Preliminary planning guidance

---

## Integration with the Larger Process

The planning process is not purely sequential. Depending on the size and complexity of the exercise, phases and steps may overlap. The key is to ensure the Exercise Concept is completed before detailed planning begins — this is the critical gateway between initial planning and detailed planning.
