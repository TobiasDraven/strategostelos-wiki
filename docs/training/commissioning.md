# Exercise Commissioning

> *"The Commissioning Instruction is a critical document that will be required to initiate the planning and design process for an exercise."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 3

## What Is Exercise Commissioning?

Exercise commissioning is the formal process by which an exercise is authorised, resourced, and initiated. It transforms a training requirement into a mandated planning activity with clear authority, objectives, and resources.

The commissioning document — variously called a **Commissioning Instruction**, **Exercise Directive**, or **Tasking Order** depending on the echelon and nation — is the single most important document in the exercise lifecycle. Without it, planning cannot begin.

---

## The Commissioning Authority

Exercises are commissioned by the appropriate authority based on the type and scale of the exercise:

| Exercise Type | Typical Commissioning Authority |
|---------------|--------------------------------|
| Service-unique | Chief of Service / Commander of Formation |
| Joint (multi-service) | Chief Joint Operations / Joint Force Commander |
| Multinational | Lead Nation / Joint Force Commander |
| National-level | National command authority / designated minister |

The commissioning authority is often referred to as the **Officer Scheduling the Exercise (OSE)** or **Convening Officer**. This individual holds the authority to initiate the exercise, appoint the Exercise Director, and allocate resources.

---

## Exercise Specification Meeting

Before the Commissioning Instruction is issued, the OSE typically convenes an **Exercise Specification Meeting** (also called an Exercise Conference or EXSPEC Conference). This meeting brings together key stakeholders to define the broad requirements:

**Attendees:**
- OSE / Convening Officer
- Prospective Exercise Director
- Senior exercise control representatives
- Resource / budget representatives

**Key outputs:**
- Exercise name and code name
- Purpose and type of exercise
- Aim and objectives (broad)
- Primary Training Audience (PTA)
- Participating and supporting forces
- Time and duration
- Location(s) and training area
- Budget envelope and resource guidance
- Key appointments
- Planning schedule and milestones
- Restrictions and constraints

## Commissioning Instruction Components

The Commissioning Instruction is a formal document that mandates the exercise and authorises planning. While formats vary across nations and organisations, the following components are standard:

### 1. Exercise Identity

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Exercise name** | Unique code name for the exercise (e.g., Exercise STEADFAST, Exercise UPLIFT) |
| **Type of exercise** | Classification (CPX, FTX, CAX, etc.) |
| **Polarity** | One-sided, two-sided, or multi-sided |
| **Level** | Strategic, operational, or tactical |

### 2. Purpose and Aim

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Why the exercise is being conducted (training, evaluation, development, deterrence) |
| **Aim** | Broad, singular statement of intent |
| **Goal** | More specific, measurable, time-bound outcome |

**Example aim:** *"To train all forces designated to form part of Exercise STEADFAST in the planning, conduct, and support of joint task force-level operations in a complex multi-domain environment."*

### 3. Exercise Objectives

Objectives are the specific, measurable outcomes the exercise must achieve. They serve as building blocks for the aim and guide all subsequent design and evaluation.

**Critical distinction:**
- **Exercise Objectives** — What the exercise must achieve overall
- **Training Objectives** — What the PTA must learn or demonstrate

Objectives must be:
- **Specific** — Unambiguous and clear
- **Measurable** — Capable of being assessed
- **Attainable** — Achievable within the exercise parameters
- **Relevant** — Aligned with the aim
- **Time-bound** — Achievable within the exercise duration
- **Inspectable** — Capable of being observed and evaluated (SMART-I)

### 4. Primary Training Audience (PTA)

The Commissioning Instruction identifies the PTA — the main participants who will be formally assessed. It may also identify Secondary Training Audience (STA) and supporting elements.

### 5. Participating and Supporting Forces

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **PTA** | Primary Training Audience (to be assessed) |
| **STA** | Secondary Training Audience (not assessed) |
| **OPFOR** | Opposing force (if applicable) |
| **Support elements** | Logistics, medical, signal, etc. |

### 6. Time and Duration

- Start and end dates
- Operational periods (if phased over months/years)
- Key milestones (conferences, reconnaissance, execution)

### 7. Location(s)

- Primary training area(s)
- Alternative or secondary locations
- Requirements for specific terrain or infrastructure

### 8. Key Appointments

| Appointment | Responsibility |
|-------------|----------------|
| **Exercise Director (EXDIR)** | Overall command of the exercise |
| **Deputy EXDIR** | Assists EXDIR (optional) |
| **Chief Exercise Control** | Controls exercise execution |
| **Force Commander (FC)** | Commands the PTA |
| **Chief Scenario** | Leads narrative and scenario development |
| **Other key posts** | As required by exercise type and scale |

### 9. Resources and Budget

- Financial authority and budget envelope
- Specific resource allocations (rations, ammunition, flying hours, vehicles)
- Any special equipment or capabilities

### 10. Planning Guidelines and Restrictions

- Dates for planning conferences (CDC, IPC, MPC, FPC)
- Planning timeline and milestones
- Specific restrictions (safety, environmental, political)
- Classification and security guidelines

## Exercise Directive vs. Commissioning Instruction

In joint and multinational contexts, the **Exercise Directive** serves the same purpose as the Commissioning Instruction but may be issued by a joint operations centre rather than a single service. The key difference is the issuing authority and the scope of the mandate:

| Document | Issuing Authority | Scope |
|----------|-------------------|-------|
| Commissioning Instruction | Single service or formation | Service-unique or single-formation exercise |
| Exercise Directive | Joint operations centre | Joint (multi-service) exercise |
| Tasking Order | Higher headquarters | Any exercise formally tasked to a subordinate |

---

## Issue and Distribution

The Commissioning Instruction must be issued as early as possible — ideally well in advance of the first planning conference. The timing depends on the size and complexity of the exercise:

| Exercise Type | Typical Lead Time |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Small tactical exercise | 3–6 months |
| Medium operational exercise | 6–12 months |
| Large joint/multinational exercise | 12–24 months |

**Distribution** is limited to key planners and is marked as **"For EXCON Eyes Only"** to prevent the PTA from gaining advance knowledge of the scenario.

---

## The Commissioning Instruction in Practice

The Commissioning Instruction is not a formality — it is the foundation of the entire exercise. A poorly written Commissioning Instruction leads to:

- Ambiguous objectives
- Misaligned design
- Resource shortfalls
- Re-work and delay
- Exercise failure

> **Critical principle:** The Commissioning Instruction must be drafted with care, issued with sufficient lead time, and contain as much detail as possible. Without it, the planning group lacks direction and the exercise deviates from the requirement.
