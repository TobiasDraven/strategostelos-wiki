# Assessment and Evaluation During Military Exercises

> *"Assessing nothing and trying to assess everything are evil twins — both are unproductive and both will hamper improvement."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 9

## Overview

Military exercises are planned and conducted to achieve specific goals and objectives. The success of the exercise is determined by the extent to which these goals and objectives are achieved. To determine whether a goal or objective has been achieved, some form of **assessment** or **evaluation** is required.

No exercise should be conducted without a credible and deliberate assessment and evaluation plan — otherwise participants will only go through the motions and the exercise becomes nothing more than a "show" or demonstration.

---

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Assessment** | An informal, non-empirical, formative process used to continuously diagnose performance. Subjective in nature; focuses on identifying strengths, weaknesses, and potential growth areas. Feedback is informal and provided directly. |
| **Evaluation** | A formal summative process directed at a specific target audience conducting a specified action under set conditions. Performance is objectively measured against a predetermined standard (outcomes-oriented). Uses specialised data collection tools; results in qualified grades, scores, or certification. |

These two processes are often used in tandem, with one supporting the other or even overlapping.

---

## Core Principle

No target group (force structure element, HQ, or individual) should be assessed or evaluated if the member or element has not been trained and certified as competent prior to the assessment or evaluation process. Assessing or evaluating untrained military personnel provides very little value, expends unnecessary resources, and wastes valuable time.

---

## Aim of Assessment and Evaluation

Assessment and evaluation should not be seen as a test or examination. Its higher purpose is to identify gaps and lessons that can be used to increase the effectiveness and performance of units, systems, and soldiers.

The aim is to provide the chain of command with objective information regarding:

- The effectiveness of training (competency, proficiency)
- The readiness level of military forces
- The functionality and effectiveness of systems, doctrine, and TTPs
- Identification of training gaps

---

## Approach to Assessment and Evaluation

### Assessment and Evaluation Agency

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Internal Assessment** | Conducted by members within the chain of command of the focus group being evaluated |
| **External Assessment** | Conducted by members or agencies outside the focus group's operational chain of command |

### Focus Group

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Collective** | All members of the group are assessed and evaluated |
| **Sample Group** | A smaller sample group represents a cross-section of the overall group |
| **Specified Focus Group** | A specific group within the larger focus group is identified for assessment |

### Focus Area

Broad focus areas include:
- Doctrine (Tactics, Techniques, Procedures, and Processes)
- Systems
- Capabilities
- Products/Outcome (End State)
- Performance

Each focus area can be broken down into levels:
- Level 1: Technical
- Level 2: Individual
- Level 3: Crew/Section
- Level 4: Sub-sub unit (Platoon)
- Level 5: Sub-Unit (Company/Combat Team)
- Level 6: Unit (Battalion/Regiment/Combat group)
- Level 7: Brigade/Formation
- Level 8: Division/Joint Task Force

### Assessment Tools

- Questionnaires and exams
- Practical demonstrations
- Assessment of products handed in
- Observation

### Assessment Standard

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Comprehensive Assessment Standards** | Large number of standards covering the total spectrum of tasks, functions, and responsibilities |
| **Selective Assessment Standard** | Only key performance areas are identified for assessment |

### Appointment of Assessors and Evaluators

Assessors and evaluators must be:
- Technically qualified to the level being assessed
- Subject matter experts (SME) in the field they will assess
- Of equivalent or higher rank than the training audience
- Operationally experienced in the aspect being assessed
- Honest, objective, able to work as part of a team
- Good interpersonal and communication skills
- Able to persevere under extreme and difficult conditions

### Grouping of Assessors and Evaluators

| System | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Pool System** | Assessors grouped in a centralised pool and tasked on an ad-hoc basis. Centralised command and control. Resource-heavy. |
| **Embedded System** | Assessors attached to specific units or sub-units. Remain with the unit for the duration. Decentralised command and control. |

**"Double-hat" appointments:** The practice of utilising a member in more than one role. Common but carries risks — should not be used to appoint members to act as controller/assessor/evaluator simultaneously during execution.

## Assessment and Evaluation Process

### Stage 2: Design and Development

- **Phase 1:** A&E assists with analysis and formulation of objectives in the Final Integrated Objective List (FIOL)
- **Phase 2:** A&E verifies and identifies potential events and incidents to be evaluated
- **Phase 3:** Injects are drafted and the Exercise Control Matrix is constructed. Expected outcomes are transformed into A&E criteria.

**Assessment and Evaluation Concept** must address:
- Focus group, capability, or individuals to be assessed
- Events and incidents to be assessed
- Assessment and evaluation standard (competency & proficiency)
- Performance indicators
- Method of data collection
- Assessment tools (grading)
- Size of A&E structure and minimum criteria of members
- Reporting line and routine
- End users for findings and recommendations

### Stage 3: Planning

**Assessment and Evaluation Plan** is drawn up in detail during the Main Planning Conference and presented during the Final Planning Conference.

**Statement of Assessment Objectives** lists:
- Objectives, incidents, or activities to be assessed
- Focus group
- Location of activity
- Timing (start time, end time, total duration)

**Assessment/Evaluation Sheet** (mark sheet) must indicate:
- Assessment standard (task, condition, required performance standard)
- Grading system

### Data Collection Methods

- Performance mark sheets based on set standards
- Observation reports
- Statements, questionnaires, lessons learned sheets
- Formative and summative evaluations
- Portfolio of Evidence (physical products)
- Hot wash-up debrief / After-action report
- Buddy ratings
- Voice recordings
- Video recordings
- Aerial photos and videos captured by UAVs
- Playback recording when using constructive simulation (CAX)

### Grading

Grading is determined in terms of:

| Measurement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Measurement of Performance (MOP)** | Determines whether the task was completed within a defined standard or condition |
| **Measurement of Effectiveness (MOE)** | Measures how effectively the task was completed |

### Grading Scales

**Example 1: Numerical Scale (0-6)**
- 0: Event not evaluated
- 1: Required to do task but did not
- 2: Task partially completed / performance unsatisfactory
- 3: Task completed with major shortcomings
- 4: Task completed with minor shortcomings
- 5: Satisfactory
- 6: Highly competent

**Example 2: Color Codes**
- Black: Not measured or not executed
- Red: Below required standard
- Amber: Meets minimum requirement but requires additional training
- Green: Meets the requirement

### Stage 4: Delivery

The bulk of performance data is collected during delivery. All members of the A&E cell must receive specialised training prior to the start of the exercise, including:
- Orientation training
- Specialised training (first aid, map reading, safety, equipment, report writing)

### Stage 5: Post-Exercise Activities and Reports

**Analysis** is the reviewing of evidence gathered to assess performance. Key components include data management and triangulation to validate findings.

**Analysis can take place:**
- **On-site:** As the incident unfolds; reporting immediately after the activity
- **Off-site:** For operational or strategic level events requiring more time

**Findings** may be published as:
- **Lessons Identified:** Recommendations based on observations relating to the discrepancy between required and actual performance
- **Lessons Learned:** A Lesson Identified that has been validated and integrated into doctrine and TTPs
