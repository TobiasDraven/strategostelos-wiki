# Military Training Vignettes

> *"A vignette is a concise description of a realistic hypothetical tactical situation — a snapshot in time that does not require a start, middle, or end."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 10

## Definition

A **vignette** (pronounced *vin-yet*) is a stand-alone training sketch derived from the French word meaning "little vine." It is defined as:

> *"A concise description of a realistic hypothetical tactical situation."*

Vignettes are often referred to as a "snapshot in time" because they do not require an elaborate back story, narrative arc, or scenario to support them. They are self-contained tactical problems that can be presented and resolved in a short period.

---

## Utilisation

Vignettes serve as a versatile training tool to introduce the training audience to a wide range of hypothetical tactical situations covering a broad spectrum of topics without the need for elaborate back story. Their versatility allows use from the tactical to strategic level.

Vignettes can be used to:

- Prepare and train forces for specific tactical situations
- Develop and refine tactical decision-making and problem-solving skills
- Assess and evaluate tactical competency of soldiers, commanders, and units
- Evaluate and assess force structures, doctrine, and TTPs (interoperability of systems)
- Provide a central reference point to compare different participants' approaches and solutions to a standard problem

---

## Considerations

Before constructing a vignette, the designer must have a clear understanding of:

- **Type of exercise** that will be conducted
- **Objective** that is to be achieved

Vignettes are best suited to support discussion-based exercises such as table-top exercises, tactical decision-making exercises, and situational training exercises. They are **not** suitable for exercises that require the planning audience to conduct elaborate and detailed operational planning, as the nature of a vignette normally allows only for minimum detail.

---

## Structural Considerations

### Perspective

Vignettes can be written from three perspectives:

| Perspective | Description | Example |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Second person** | Uses "You" to address the training audience directly | "You have been appointed to..." |
| **Third person** | Uses "he, she, it, they" | "The Army has realised that..." |
| **Neutral** | Object is given a name or identity | "Cyclone Juliet has..." / "451 Battalion is..." |

### Vantage Point

This refers to the position from which the vignette is drafted:

- **Own Forces vantage point:** "Platoon 3 of C-Coy, 1 South African Infantry Battalion has been tasked to..."
- **Enemy vantage point:** "The enemy has deployed a large force in..."

### Time Frame

Three time frames can be used, independently or jointly:

| Time Frame | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Future dated** | The incident is still to take place; focus on prevention or mitigation |
| **Present time** | The incident is taking place right now |
| **Past/Completed** | The incident has occurred; consequence management required |

> **Recommendation:** Stay within a single time frame per vignette. Should a different time frame be required, draft a new vignette.

---

## Construction

The success of a vignette lies in proper construction, composed of two parts:

### Part 1: Situation Sketch

Sets the scene and describes the **when, where, who, and what**:

| Element | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **When** | Time and date | "At 07:30 on 15 May..." / "On 29 June at 03:30..." |
| **Where** | Location or position | "On the corner of Radebe and Collins Road" / "At grid Ref 987 563" |
| **Who** | Role players involved | "39 Bn Group, strengthened with a squadron of armoured cars and a battery of 120mm mortars..." |
| **What** | Action(s) the OPFOR is conducting | Detailed description of activities, aim, and intention |

### Part 2: Expected Outcome

Stipulates the mission and expected outcome. The expected outcome must clearly indicate the required **task**, **condition**, and **standard** to be achieved.

---

## Restrictions and Limitations

Restrictions and limitations may be included as part of the problem statement:

- **Restrictions:** Inhibitors over which you have no control (legal prescripts, Law of Armed Conflict, Status of Forces Agreement)
- **Limitations:** Inhibitors that are self-imposed (Rules of Engagement, force strength, weapon types allocated)

---

## Training Aids

To enhance the training experience when using vignettes, the use of training aids is encouraged:

- Field training under simulated operational conditions
- Role players in the form of OPFOR (Real-life Simulation)
- Computer simulation (Constructive or Virtual)
- Sand models
- Maps and aerial photos

---

## Daisy-Chaining Technique

Vignettes can be used as individual building blocks to construct a larger exercise by "daisy-chaining" a number of vignettes together to create a series of sequential operational events or incidents.

| Technique | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Three-Phased Vignette** | Past incident → current events → future planning requirement |
| **Linear Phased Timeline** | Vignettes follow one another sequentially on a linear timeline |
| **Exploration of Random Possibilities** | Vignettes scattered across locations within an AO; exposes PTA to diverse challenges and prevents exercise fatigue |
| **Phased Escalation/De-escalation** | Complexity increases or decreases as the exercise progresses |
