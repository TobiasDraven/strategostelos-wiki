# Site Reconnaissance

> *"Once the concept is drafted, a physical site reconnaissance confirms that the training area can support the planned events."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 7

## Why Conduct a Site Reconnaissance?

A physical site reconnaissance is essential to confirm that the training area can support the planned events. The Exercise Concept may look sound on paper, but only a physical visit can reveal whether the terrain, infrastructure, and local conditions will actually allow the exercise to be executed as designed.

The reconnaissance serves several purposes:
- Confirm terrain suitability for planned incidents
- Identify infrastructure requirements and constraints
- Assess safety risks and environmental sensitivities
- Establish liaison with local authorities
- Identify required adjustments to the concept

---

## Reconnaissance Team Composition

The reconnaissance team should include representatives from all key EXCON functions:

| Role | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Chief Real-Life Support / Exercise Warrant Officer** | Accommodation, facilities, sustainment |
| **Chief Scenario Development** | Terrain suitability for planned incidents |
| **Chief Exercise Control** | Control positions, communications, observation |
| **Battlefield Preparation / Simulation Representative** | Live-fire areas, simulation infrastructure |
| **Signal Officer** | Communications footprint, cell coverage |
| **Medical Officer** | Medical facility access, evacuation routes |
| **Additional members as required** | Liaison, safety, environmental |

---

## Reconnaissance Focus Areas

### Terrain

- Suitability for the type of exercise (size, geography, vegetation, hydrography)
- Time and distance between key locations
- Natural and man-made obstacles
- Potential incident sites
- Control and observation positions

### Infrastructure

- Office space, mess facilities, ablutions, accommodation
- Power supply, water, sewerage
- Road networks and access routes
- Communications infrastructure

### Restrictions

- Declared grave sites, heritage sites, environmentally sensitive areas
- Legislation regarding noise, open fires, flying hours
- Protected areas and wildlife
- Land ownership and access rights

### Safety

- Live-fire areas and minimum safe distances
- Risk of veldfires, civilian presence, and livestock
- Evacuation routes and medical facility access
- Potential hazard areas (cliffs, water bodies, contaminated ground)

### Liaison

- Military controlling authority for the training area
- Local and municipal structures (Mayor, Executive Councils, tribal chiefs)
- Security structures (Police, Traffic Department, local security forums)
- Emergency response institutions
- Landowners and local communities

---

## Back Brief

On completion, the reconnaissance team presents a back brief to the Exercise Director (EXDIR) and planning team covering:

- **Site suitability assessment** — Does the terrain support the planned incidents?
- **Real-life support implications** — What facilities and infrastructure are available? What must be brought in?
- **Required adjustments to the concept** — What must change based on what was found?

If the site is unsuitable, the team recommends:
- Selection of an alternative venue
- Adjustment of the exercise concept (with EXDIR approval)
- Postponement or cancellation
