# The Main Events List — MEL

> *"The MEL is the structural skeleton of the exercise. It translates the FIOL's thematic groupings into a sequence of operational events that collectively address every objective."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 7

## What Is the MEL?

The **Main Events List (MEL)** is the primary structural document in exercise design. It translates the Final Integrated Objective List (FIOL) into a sequence of operational events that collectively provide the Primary Training Audience (PTA) with opportunities to demonstrate the required competencies.

The MEL is the bridge between objectives and execution — it ensures every objective is addressed by at least one event, and every event traces back to a specific objective.

---

## Construction Method

The MEL is constructed in four sequential steps:

### Step 1: Identify Themes

Review the FIOL and identify common operational themes. A **theme** is a central idea or functional area that groups related objectives.

**Standard themes** (adapt to your operational context):

| Theme | Example Objectives |
|-------|-------------------|
| Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR) | Conduct tactical questioning; produce intelligence estimate |
| Operational Planning | Produce operations order; conduct staff estimate |
| Conduct of Operations / Decision Making | Execute tactical manoeuvre; adjust plan during execution |
| Support and Sustainment | Resupply under fire; coordinate casualty evacuation |
| Medical Support | Establish Role 1 medical facility; conduct CASEVAC |
| Force Protection | Detect and counter IED; implement base defence plan |
| Air Support / Operations | Coordinate close air support; conduct aerial resupply |
| Maritime Support / Operations | Conduct coastal patrol; board suspicious vessel |
| Civil-Military Cooperation (CIMIC) | Coordinate with local authorities; conduct hearts-and-minds operation |
| Logistics | Establish distribution system; recover damaged vehicles |

> **Critical rule:** Your themes must derive from your FIOL, not from a template. The themes above are illustrative only.

### Step 2: Sequence Themes

Order themes in the sequence in which they would occur during a realistic operation. This creates a logical operational flow and serves as the backbone of the exercise timeline.

**Exception:** At the tactical decision-making level (e.g., Staff Exercises, Tactical Training Exercises), theme sequencing may not reflect operational sequence. In such cases, sequence by decision-making priority or functional dependency.

### Step 3: Decompose Themes into Events

For each theme, identify the specific events that will exercise the associated objectives.

**Event definition:** A planned activity that consists of one or more incidents and will normally be of a tactical nature. Events are the main building blocks of an exercise and are used during the construction of the exercise concept. *(Adapted from NATO AJP-3, Annex C)*

### Step 4: Link Events to Objectives

For each event, specify which FIOL objective(s) it addresses. This ensures traceability and prevents design drift.

## MEL Format

| Theme | Event | Objective(s) / Comments |
|-------|-------|-------------------------|
| 1. ISR | 1.1 Route reconnaissance | Obj 2: Conduct tactical reconnaissance |
| 1. ISR | 1.2 Intelligence preparation | Obj 3: Produce IPB |
| 2. Planning | 2.1 Staff estimate | Obj 4: Conduct operational estimate |
| 2. Planning | 2.2 OPORD production | Obj 5: Produce operations order |
| 3. Conduct | 3.1 Execute ambush | Obj 6: Execute tactical manoeuvre |
| 3. Conduct | 3.2 Adjust plan during execution | Obj 7: Adapt plan under pressure |

**Formatting rules:**
- Themes are numbered sequentially (1, 2, 3...)
- Events are numbered within themes (1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 2.2...)
- Events are stated crisply: one to five words
- Multiple objectives may be linked to a single event

---

## Quality Criteria

A well-constructed MEL:

- ✅ Addresses every objective in the FIOL at least once
- ✅ Contains no events that cannot be traced to an objective
- ✅ Is sequenced in a logically coherent operational flow
- ✅ Is realistic given the available time, space, and forces

**Quality check:** If you cannot trace every element of your exercise design back to a specific objective in the FIOL, your design is unfocused.

---

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Consequence | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Events not linked to objectives | Design drift; unfocused exercise | Every event must reference at least one FIOL objective |
| Too many events | Exercise becomes rushed or superficial | Prioritise; limit events to what can realistically be executed |
| Themes not derived from FIOL | Misalignment with training objectives | Always start with the FIOL, not a template |
| Unrealistic sequencing | Loss of credibility; poor training value | Validate sequence against operational logic |
