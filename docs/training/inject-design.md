# Inject Design and Construction

> *"An inject is a pre-determined and pre-planned input directed at a specific element of the PTA, designed to act as a trigger to initiate a specific incident."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 7

## What Is an Inject?

An **inject** is the fundamental unit of exercise execution. It is a pre-determined and pre-planned input directed at a specific element of the Primary Training Audience (PTA), designed to act as a trigger to initiate a specific incident. It introduces or confronts the training audience with a situation that requires a response or reaction. The response is measured against a predetermined competency standard.

**Key characteristics:**
- **Pre-determined:** Injects are planned in advance, not improvised during execution
- **Pre-planned:** Injects are aligned with the MIL and Exercise Concept
- **Directed:** Each inject targets a specific PTA element
- **Triggering:** Each inject initiates or sustains an incident
- **Measurable:** The PTA response is assessed against a standard

---

## Inject Entry Points

Injects enter the exercise through different channels depending on the desired effect:

| Entry Point | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Ground Level (Bottom-Up)** | Inject enters at the tactical level, initiated by lower-echelon controllers or simulation systems |
| **HQ Level (Top-Down)** | Inject enters at headquarters level, initiated by higher-echelon controllers |
| **Direct / Focused Insertion** | Inject is delivered directly to a specific PTA element by the controller, bypassing normal command channels |

---

## Inject Formats

Injects can be delivered in multiple formats, depending on the exercise type and the incident being triggered:

| Format | Description | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Written** | Written orders, messages, or intelligence reports delivered to PTA | OPORD fragment, INTSUM, situation report |
| **System Input** | Inject delivered through simulation systems (C4I, VBS, JCATS) | Digital message in C4I system |
| **Verbal Input** | Verbal message delivered by controller or role-player | Radio transmission, face-to-face briefing |
| **Audio-Visual** | Video or audio recording presented to PTA | News broadcast, surveillance footage |
| **Live Simulation (Enactment)** | Physical enactment by role-players or OPFOR | Civilian protest, ambush, IED discovery |

## Principles of Inject Design

### Sufficiency

An inject must contain enough information for the PTA to understand the situation and respond meaningfully. Every inject must answer:

- **When** is it happening?
- **Where** is it happening?
- **What** is happening / what will happen / what happened?
- **Who** is involved (own forces and OPFOR)?
- **Tasking** — what is required, by whom, and by when?

**Example inject:**

> *"At 02:35 on 23 February 2022, eight nautical miles east of Richards Bay, the Maltese-registered merchant vessel Pisces Dawn was attacked by three motorboats carrying armed men. The men seized control of the vessel, robbed the crew of personal items, and are holding the captain and crew at ransom. A joint task group consisting of 4 Special Forces Regiment, the Maritime Reaction Squadron, a SA Navy Inshore Patrol Vessel, and two Oryx helicopters has been assembled. C SANDF has tasked the grouping to plan and present a detailed plan for the release of hostages by 250900B Feb 22, addressing both opposed and unopposed boarding options."*

### Escalation

Some incidents require multiple injects to develop fully. The principle of escalation applies when follow-up injects introduce additional detail, changing conditions, or complications that sustain and deepen the incident.

**Example escalation:**

| Inject | Content | PTA Response |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| 1 | IED reported on Route BLUE | PTA conducts route clearance |
| 2 | Second IED discovered 500m further along route | PTA adjusts movement plan |
| 3 | Civilian casualty from second IED | PTA coordinates CASEVAC and media management |

Each inject in the sequence escalates the incident, maintaining cognitive load and testing additional competencies.

## Inject Numbering

Injects are numbered to maintain traceability to their parent event and incident:

| Inject No. | Event | Incident |
|------------|-------|----------|
| 1.1.1-I1 | 1.1 | 1.1.1 |
| 1.1.2-I1 | 1.1 | 1.1.2 |
| 1.2.1-I1 | 1.2 | 1.2.1 |

This numbering system becomes the reference framework for the Exercise Control Matrix and Inject Control Sheets.

---

## Quality Criteria

A well-constructed inject:

- ✅ Is clear, concise, and sufficient
- ✅ Contains all required information (when, where, what, who, tasking)
- ✅ Is linked to a specific incident in the MIL
- ✅ Is linked to a specific objective in the FIOL
- ✅ Has assessment criteria linked to specific FIOL objectives
- ✅ Has safety considerations that address real risks
- ✅ Has contingency options for common PTA failure modes
