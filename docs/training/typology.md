# Typology of Military Exercises

> *"War is expensive in terms of funds, material, and human lives. Its destructive nature means that real-world experience is usually limited. The only way for soldiers, sailors, and airmen to prepare for battle is by conducting and participating in realistic military exercises."*
> — Adapted from EXDESIGN Textbook, Chapter 1

## Definition

A **military exercise** is a training activity, manoeuvre, or simulated wartime operation conducted under simulated operational conditions involving planning, preparation, and execution. It is carried out for the purpose of training and evaluation of military personnel and capabilities.[^1]

Military exercises are the bridge between theory and practice. They allow commanders and staffs to test procedures, validate plans, rehearse decision-making, and identify capability gaps — without the cost and risk of actual combat.

---

## Purpose of Military Exercises

Exercises serve multiple purposes, often simultaneously:

| Purpose | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Training** | Provide opportunities to practise competencies, train in new doctrine or TTPs, enhance interoperability, and develop problem-solving skills |
| **Evaluation** | Assess fighting ability, staff competencies, doctrine, structures, procedures, and equipment |
| **Force Preparation** | Prepare and rehearse forces for operations or specific missions (combat readiness and mission readiness training) |
| **Development** | Serve as experimental testbeds for new TTPs, doctrine, and equipment |
| **Deterrence** | Demonstrate preparedness and capabilities as a show of force |
| **Tool of Government** | Provide governments with a mechanism for influence and foreign policy |

---

## Classification by Participation Structure

Military exercises can be classified by the participating force structure:

### Service-Unique Exercise
An exercise consisting of one or more force structure elements from a single service or division. Example: An army-only field training exercise.

### Joint Exercise
An exercise in which two or more force structure elements of two or more services participate jointly. Example: A combined army-navy-air force command post exercise.

### Multinational Exercise (Combined Exercise)
An exercise involving the armed forces of two or more countries. Example: NATO's *Steadfast Defender* series.

### Joint Inter-Departmental, Inter-Agency, Multi-National Exercise (JI²M)
An exercise involving two or more services, one or more government departments or agencies, and military or government departments from a foreign country. This is the most complex classification, typical of whole-of-government crisis response exercises.

---

## Classification by Level

Exercises are conducted at different levels of warfare, which determines the type of exercise appropriate:

| Level | Typical Echelon | Exercise Focus |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Strategic** | National command authority, combatant command | Strategic decision-making, policy, campaign design |
| **Operational** | Joint task force, division and larger | Operational planning, sustainment, coordination |
| **Tactical** | Brigade and below | Tactical execution, combined arms, decision-making |
| **Technical** | Units and individuals | Procedures, systems, communications |

The tactical level is further sub-divided into individual, section/crew, platoon, company, battalion, and brigade levels.

---

## Training Audience

Every exercise is directed at a specific training audience:

### Primary Training Audience (PTA)
The main participants who physically participate in the exercise. The aim and objectives are directed at this group. Their performance is measured, assessed, and evaluated by exercise control (EXCON). The PTA does not form part of the design team.

### Secondary Training Audience (STA)
Elements that assist EXCON in directing and executing events. STAs may exploit ad hoc training opportunities but are not formally assessed. Examples include directing staff at training institutions and logistical support units.

### Elements in Support
Supporting elements not directly involved in the exercise but providing overall support (e.g., base support, medical, transport).

---

## Polarity

Polarity refers to the number of opposing sides in an exercise:

- **One-sided:** Only the PTA is represented. The opposing side is simulated or virtual. Most command post exercises are one-sided.
- **Two-sided:** Two sides oppose each other. Common in tactical exercises with a live OPFOR.
- **Multi-sided:** More than two sides are involved, often including non-military actors. Typical of complex stability operations or multi-agency exercises.

## Types of Military Exercises

The selection of the correct exercise type is crucial to achieving exercise objectives. The following types are recognised across international doctrine:

### Discussion-Based Exercises

| Type | Description | Typical Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Study Period / Group** | Case study analysis, critical discussion, staff papers | Develop critical thinking, broaden understanding |
| **Staff Ride** | Visit to historical battle sites for terrain analysis | Terrain analysis, decision-making in context |
| **Model Exercise (Sand Model)** | Tactical problems examined using models on terrain representation | Teach theory and tactics in modified space/time |
| **Map Exercise (MAPEX)** | Conducted on a map; staff planning, coordination, decision-making | Staff training at battalion level and above |
| **Table Top Exercise (TTX)** | Discussion-based; scenarios analysed to confirm roles, evaluate procedures | Identify gaps, confirm responsibilities, enhance understanding |
| **War Gaming** | Simulated military training event using real or imaginary problems | Force-on-force simulation, optimisation, tactical decision-making |

### Operations-Based Exercises

| Type | Description | Typical Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Command Post Exercise (CPX)** | HQ-focused; simulated forces; commanders and staff | Staff procedures, C2 systems, decision-making |
| **Field Training Exercise (FTX)** | Live execution with troops and equipment under simulated conditions | Tactical proficiency, combined arms integration |
| **Live-Fire Exercise (LFX)** | Live ammunition and ordnance; manoeuvre and combat drills | Combat skills, combined arms live-fire integration |
| **Situational Training Exercise (STX)** | Focuses on specific events, tasks, or techniques | Standalone or part of larger FTX |
| **Command Field Exercise (CFX)** | Hybrid CPX + reduced live execution | C2 under semi-realistic conditions |
| **Computer-Assisted Exercise (CAX)** | Computer simulation of operations between opposing forces | Large-scale operations, joint/combined training |
| **Tactical Exercise Without Troops (TEWT)** | No troops; commanders and staff apply theory to procedures | Planning and decision-making training |

### Force Preparation Exercises

| Type | Description | Typical Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Combat Readiness Exercise (CRT)** | Prepare and evaluate combat readiness of a unit or force | Service-unique readiness validation |
| **Mission Readiness Exercise (MRT)** | Prepare a unit for deployment to a specific mission | Pre-deployment rehearsal |
| **Mission Rehearsal Exercise (MRX)** | Rehearse a specific named operation as part of pre-deployment training | Final preparation before deployment |

## Exercise Integration

Exercise integration combines multiple smaller exercises of different types into a single larger, more complex exercise. This allows:

- Integration of different exercise types
- Accommodation of different levels of military operations
- A larger mix of forces exercising together
- Geographical dispersal of exercise events
- Full operational flow with different phases conducted simultaneously

---

## Progressive Training Approach

A practical approach begins with simple exercises and progressively increases scope, duration, and complexity. Each exercise should build on the previous one:

1. **TTX** → Discuss roles and solutions (low resources)
2. **MAPEX / Model Exercise** → Apply to enhanced scenario
3. **CPX + CAX** → Commander and full HQ staff participate
4. **FTX / CFX** → Evaluate under field conditions

This progression ensures foundations are solid before complexity increases.

---

## Selecting the Correct Type

The selection of exercise type depends on:

- **Training audience** — Who must be trained?
- **Objectives** — What must be achieved?
- **Level** — Strategic, operational, or tactical?
- **Resources** — Budget, time, forces, equipment available?
- **Polarity** — One-sided, two-sided, or multi-sided?
- **Complexity** — How much complexity is needed to achieve the objectives?

> **Critical principle:** The exercise type must match the objectives. A poorly chosen exercise type will not achieve the objectives, regardless of how well it is executed.
