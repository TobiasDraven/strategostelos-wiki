    Battalion Staff Sensemaking — The Cognitive Architecture of Command¶

    "The battle staff is that element within the organisation which helps the commander create and refine the battlefield image." — Kahan, Battle Staff Integration, 1994

    Organisational Cognition at Battalion Level¶

    A major serves as a company commander or battalion staff officer. At this level, individual sensemaking is necessary but not sufficient. The battalion must make sense of the battlefield as an organisation. The staff processes information from multiple companies. The commander blends that information with experience and judgement. Together, they form a shared picture that no single person could build alone.

    This page covers the noetic work of battalion command. It focuses on how a staff builds and maintains a shared understanding of the battlefield, and how a major contributes to that process.

    The Staff as Cognitive System¶

    Battle Tracking¶

    Battle tracking is the process of developing and refining a shared image of the battlefield. The staff collects, processes, analyses, and monitors information. The commander uses that image to make decisions and focus combat power. Effective battle tracking requires:

    • Information sharing across functions. Intelligence, operations, and logistics must share what they know. Stove-pipes kill shared understanding.
    • A common reference point. The common operational picture (COP) gives everyone the same frame of reference.
    • Mutual awareness. Each staff officer must understand what the others need to know.

    The Battle Rhythm¶

    The battle rhythm synchronises staff activities. It sets a cycle of meetings, briefings, and updates. The standard rhythm includes:

    • Commander's update briefing. The S2 and S3 brief the current situation.
    • Operations update. Subordinate units report their status.
    • Plans meeting. The staff develops or refines courses of action.
    • Targeting meeting. The fire support team synchronises fires.

    But rigid battle rhythms can fail. When operations accelerate, meetings become impossible. The staff must adapt. A flexible battle rhythm compresses or expands as the fight demands. The commander and staff must maintain shared understanding through running estimates and informal processes when formal meetings are not possible.

    The S2/S3 Relationship¶

    Intelligence and Operations as a Cognitive Partnership¶

    The S2 (intelligence officer) and S3 (operations officer) must work as a team. The S2 focuses on the enemy. The S3 focuses on friendly operations. Neither can succeed alone.

    The S2's cognitive work: - Conducts intelligence preparation of the battlefield (IPB). - Develops enemy courses of action and situation templates. - Identifies high-value targets and named areas of interest. - Answers the commander's priority intelligence requirements.

    The S3's cognitive work: - Develops friendly courses of action. - Synchronises the operations order. - Coordinates fire support, engineer assets, and manoeuvre. - Tracks the execution of operations.

    Together, they wargame friendly plans against enemy plans. They identify decision points. They build the synchronisation matrix.

    The S2 Must Drive Scout Planning¶

    The S2 must give the scout platoon precise guidance. The scout platoon leader needs to know what to look for, where to look, and when to report. The S2 develops the reconnaissance and surveillance (R&S) plan. The S3 translates that plan into operational terms.

    The Commander's Critical Information Requirements¶

    What the Commander Needs to Know¶

    The commander sets the CCIR — the information needed to make decisions. The staff must understand what the commander needs and why. The CCIR drives the collection plan. It focuses scarce reconnaissance assets on the most important questions.

    Common CCIR at battalion level include: - Will the enemy counterattack? - Where is the enemy main effort? - What is the status of the adjacent unit's sector? - Are the bridges on the main supply route intact?

    The staff must anticipate the commander's information needs. Good staff officers think ahead. They provide the commander with the information needed for the next decision, not just the current one.

    The Common Operational Picture¶

    Building the COP¶

    The COP is the shared visual display of the battlefield. It shows friendly positions, enemy positions, and control measures. The S2 builds the enemy picture. The S3 builds the friendly picture. Together they create the fused COP.

    At battalion level, the COP comes from many sources: - Spot reports from subordinate units. - Sensor feeds (UAV, radar, signals intelligence). - Reports from adjacent units. - Intelligence reach from higher headquarters.

    The S2 fuses these inputs. The result is a picture that is never complete. The S2 must confirm or deny enemy courses of action. They must fill gaps in knowledge.

    The Limits of the COP¶

    The COP is a model, not reality. It is always incomplete. Sometimes it is wrong. The staff must understand its limits:

    • Friendly reports are more reliable than enemy reports. Friendly units know their own positions. Enemy positions are estimates.
    • The enemy COP is the hardest to build. Enemy icons on the COP represent the best available assessment, not confirmed fact.
    • Redundancy must be removed. Multiple observers may report the same enemy unit. The S2 must eliminate duplicate icons.

    The Major's Cognitive Work¶

    As Company Commander¶

    As a company commander, you are the primary sensemaker for your company. You receive the battalion commander's intent. You develop your company concept. You coordinate with adjacent companies. You maintain awareness of the battalion situation one level higher.

    Your cognitive work includes: - Understanding the battalion end state. What must the battalion achieve? - Translating intent into company orders. What must your company do? - Maintaining situational awareness. What is happening in adjacent sectors? - Making decisions in contact. When the plan changes, you must adapt.

    As Battalion Staff Officer¶

    As a staff officer (S2 or S3), you support the battalion commander's sensemaking. You process information. You develop products. You anticipate needs. You coordinate with the adjacent staff sections.

    Your cognitive work includes: - Running estimates. You maintain a continuous estimate of the situation in your functional area. - Briefing the commander. You distil complex information into clear assessments. - Coordinating with peers. You share information with the other staff sections. - Driving the collection plan. You task reconnaissance assets to answer the commander's questions.

    Distributed Sensemaking¶

    The Battalion as a Cognitive Network¶

    The battalion is a network of sensemakers. Each company commander interprets the situation in their sector. Each staff officer interprets it through their functional lens. The commander fuses these interpretations into a coherent picture.

    Research on distributed sensemaking identifies nine principles for supporting it in military teams. The most important for battalion staff are:

    1. Represent information quality and provenance. Show what is known, what is suspected, and what is templated.
    2. Promote expertise. Each staff officer must be the expert in their area.
    3. Minimise the cost of achieving common ground. Make it easy for staff to share information and reach shared understanding.
    4. Provide explanatory context. Do not just report facts. Explain what they mean.

    Training the Battalion Staff¶

    Staff Exercises¶

    Train the staff through realistic exercises. Use scenarios that require information sharing, assessment, and decision-making. Debrief the process, not only the product. Ask: What did the staff miss? Why? How can we improve?

    Build Intelligence Literacy¶

    At battalion level, the staff is small enough to train for intelligence literacy. Every staff officer should understand the IPB process, the collection plan, and the intelligence estimate. The S2 cannot do everything alone. The whole staff must think about the enemy.
